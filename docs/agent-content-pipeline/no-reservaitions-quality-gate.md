# No Reservaitions Quality Gate

Use this as the final editorial pass before publishing a No Reservaitions review.

## Required before drafting

- Resolve each user-mentioned dish or drink against the official menu or best corroborated source when possible.
- Capture canonical item names and useful components for resolved dishes.
- Build `narrativeContext` with at least two grounded anchors across firsthand memory and sourced public context.
- Research narrative material—not just metadata—including venue or building history, owners, neighborhood or regional role, and why the stop made sense that day.
- Mark missing firsthand context explicitly.
- If `followUpNeeded` is true, ask Dan one compact question and stop before drafting.
- Ensure `prompt.md` contains both `## Narrative anchors` and `## Dan-approved calibration`.

## Draft must do

- Read like a remembered experience, not an agent summary.
- Put the reader in a specific place or moment.
- Mention at least two concrete specifics when the evidence provides them.
- Connect food or drink to people, place, history, appetite, or occasion.
- Use venue history only when it changes how the visit feels.
- Carry a distinct judgment that belongs to this exact visit.

## Swap-name test

Replace the venue name mentally with another restaurant. If most of the review still works, reject it. The draft needs more concrete scene, local context, sensory memory, or human stakes.

## Draft must not do

- Mention photos, images, screenshots, prompts, packets, research, or publishing.
- Invent atmosphere, flavor, texture, service, dialogue, reactions, ownership, or history.
- Substitute abstract order-analysis for lived experience—for example, making dishes into rival theories, arguments, strategies, or cases without a scene to support it.
- Flatten composed dishes into vague nouns.
- Force a caveat, trade-off, “who should go,” or practical takeaway.
- Reuse stock openings, closings, calibration wording, or obvious agent filler.
- Sound like a compliance report about evidence handling.

## Dan-approved calibration

Dan's revisions of Dutch Maid Bakery & Cafe and High Point Restaurant are the current standard. Their useful qualities are:

- immediate sense of place
- conversational appetite
- sensory and atmospheric specificity
- humor with a point of view
- food connected to the people or occasion
- an ending that explains why the memory mattered

Calibrate to those qualities without copying their wording, cadence, or structure. Any sensory or atmospheric detail must still be grounded.

## Fast failure phrases

If these appear as generic filler rather than earned language, stop and rewrite:

- “The photo tells you”
- “The image makes the case”
- “two theories of…”
- “made a useful sequence”
- “made their separate cases”
- “gave the order some lift”
- “sounds like a very solid move”
- “if you want X, this is/isn't your room”

## Publish threshold

Do not publish unless the draft feels like somebody who was there remembering why the stop mattered. Correct metadata, polished abstraction, and a menu recap are not enough.
