Zanies is familiar enough now that these nights belong to the comedian, not the room. Friday was Sam Morril: the same low stage, tight tables, blue-purple light, and empty microphone waiting beneath a wall of faces that had already survived the place. Before the doors, we pregamed with THC/CBD drinks in public, having learned—at least as we understood it—that nothing in Tennessee law barred consuming them that way. A modest little civic revelation, swallowed cold.

Inside, the obligation to order became a bucket of State Park Blondes. Tennessee Brew Works makes it light and crisp, a 4.5-percent beer built for repetition rather than contemplation. That is exactly what a comedy-club bucket should be: local, uncomplicated, and still cold when the next joke arrives.

Gary Vider opened, introduced less like hired support than Morril's buddy, and he was genuinely great. Openers are often treated as the toll paid before the name on the ticket appears. Vider made the room feel lucky to have shown up early.

Then Morril killed. The phrase gets thrown around until it means little, but here it fit: the room was his, the beer kept moving, and the night never sagged. Familiar venue, unexpectedly strong opener, lethal headliner—another Zanies visit worth filing under the person who made it matter.
