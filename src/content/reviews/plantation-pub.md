Two nine-hole rounds of golf will teach you a lot about patience, mostly that you have less of it than you thought. Afterward the legs want a stool, the soul wants something cold, and nobody wants to talk about the back nine. Plantation Pub, out in Bellevue, turned out to be exactly the place to stop asking anything of the day.

It's a neighborhood bar, decorated by accumulation rather than design. A 250th anniversary banner, a Coors Light sign cheering "Go Vawls," a Red Bull fridge behind the bar like it pays rent. It's the stuff a room collects when the same people keep coming back, and it makes you feel like one of them before the first pour.

A Guinness and a Coors Banquet at the bar: a sport coat and a work shirt, and both fit. The Guinness asks you to slow down. The Banquet asks nothing of anyone, which after a few hours of chasing a small white ball through the grass is a quality I've come to admire in a beer.

The 49ers and Cowboys were on the big screen, more football on the TVs around it, and the place had that Sunday murmur where nobody's in a hurry to be anywhere else. Somewhere in there I said, "This is wonderful." That's not a line you plan. It comes out when the beer is cold, the game is on, and your legs have finally stopped arguing with you.

Eighteen holes the long way around, a stool under a Vols sign, and an afternoon that didn't need to be anything more than it was. I meant every word of it.
