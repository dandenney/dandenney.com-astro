Some rooms were built for music. Others seem to have been built for something closer to communion. The Ryman is the latter. A century of voices has soaked into those wooden pews, and when the right band takes the stage, you can almost feel the building listening along with everyone else.

Brother Bird and Kevin Devine made for a wonderful opening combination, setting the evening in motion with the kind of intimate, thoughtful songwriting that belongs in a room like this. No need for spectacle when the songs themselves have something worth saying. A few Chicken Scratch beers kept things properly Nashville, cold and uncomplicated, while the evening gradually moved toward something considerably more emotional.

Manchester Orchestra arrived carrying the weight of a difficult year, but whatever they brought with them, they poured it into the music. There's an extraordinary tension in their songs, the way tenderness and devastation can occupy the same few minutes. Andy Hull's voice can sound like a confession one moment and a desperate attempt to reach heaven the next. At the Ryman, every fragile note and explosive crescendo had somewhere to go.

And then there was "I Know How to Speak" and "The Silence." Two songs that already carry enormous emotional weight, performed with a beauty I couldn't have imagined beforehand. The kind of performances that make you forget about the people sitting around you, the beer in your hand, and everything waiting outside those doors. For a few minutes, there's nothing but the music and whatever it manages to pull out of you.

Concerts are often measured by energy, production, or how many favorite songs made the setlist. This one deserved a different measure. It was beautiful in the way that only something honest and a little wounded can be. The Ryman has witnessed more than its share of extraordinary music. Manchester Orchestra gave those old walls another night worth remembering.
