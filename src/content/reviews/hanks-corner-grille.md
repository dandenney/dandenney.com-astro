Some restaurants arrive with a concept. Hank's Corner Grille arrives with a corner, a grille, and enough confidence to leave the rest alone. In Tracy City, the order was equally direct: a burger, a Philly Cheese, fries, and two Cokes. It was less a tasting strategy than an agreement that the meal had already spent enough time thinking about itself.

The burger held down the classic end of that agreement. A sesame-seed bun, a red basket, fries alongside it, Coke within reach. The details beneath the bun could remain private. “Burger” was enough information, one of those rare words that functions as both a category and a complete plan.

The Philly Cheese made the busier case. Thinly sliced steak, onions, peppers, and mozzarella filled the bread, with another run of fries occupying the basket. Where the burger kept its structure to itself, the Philly spread the whole argument across the table: chopped, layered, and barely interested in containment.

Two baskets and two Cokes left very little room for ceremony, which was precisely the point. Hank's did not ask the meal to become a story before it could do its job. It gave each order a name that meant something, put fries beside it, and trusted clarity to carry the rest. Sometimes the pleasure is not discovery. Sometimes it is getting exactly the nouns you ordered.
