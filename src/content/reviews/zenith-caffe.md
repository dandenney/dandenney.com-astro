Zenith Caffè belongs to the branch of brunch that long ago stopped pretending virtue was part of the deal. In Matosinhos, with sea air never all that far away and enough lighter coastal options nearby to make self-restraint available if you want it, the move here was a chicken toast and a cappuccino. That turns out to be a very clear declaration that self-restraint can wait.

The toast arrives looking less assembled than draped. Thick bread carries the load, but what announces itself first is the molten cheese, sliding over the edges in a way that suggests the kitchen understands excess as a visual language. Underneath is roast chicken with enough heft to keep the whole thing from collapsing into gimmick, while the garnish of dried tomato and strawberry gives the plate a strange little flourish that somehow makes perfect sense in a room committed to brunch as performance.

What keeps it from becoming merely theatrical is that Zenith seems to know exactly what kind of appetite it is serving. The official build of the house toast leans on chicken, cheddar, bacon, fried egg, francesinha sauce, and chives, which explains why the plate eats more like a full meal than a coffee-shop prop. This is the sort of order that borrows the all-day optimism of brunch, then quietly smuggles in the deeper logic of hangover food, bar food, and lunch without asking permission from any of them.

That is where the cappuccino earns its place. Coffee in a setting like this can easily become decoration, something to make the table look complete while the food does all the real talking. Here it feels functional in the best way: the sober, bitter note that cuts through the richness and keeps the meal from tipping fully into parody. Zenith Caffè ends up making a persuasive case for brunch that knows spectacle is part of the pleasure and has the good sense to back it with enough weight to matter.
