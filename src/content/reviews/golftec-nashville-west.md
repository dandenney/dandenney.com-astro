What sells the experience is not fake country-club atmosphere or some fantasy that a simulator bay can replace a real round. It is the nerdiness. When a 7-iron shot shows up as 39 yards with a face-to-target number you cannot sweet-talk away, the room stops being a sales floor and starts feeling like a lab for your bad habits.

That kind of honesty is weirdly addictive. Water was the only drink, which felt right. Nothing here needs lubrication or ceremony. For people wired to enjoy dashboards, diagnostics, and visible feedback loops, that is a legitimate good time.

The trade-off is obvious: there is no breeze, no grass, no lucky bounce, none of the accidental romance that makes a real course feel alive.
