We went to Bridgestone Arena to see The Shins and Silversun Pickups again. That was the plan. Weezer was the headliner, technically, but at first they felt almost like the administrative detail attached to getting two bands we already knew we wanted back in front of us.

The Shins played beneath an enormous blue field of illustrated fruit and curling vines, their name towering over the stage. In an arena built to make everything feel oversized, it was a strange little garden for a band whose songs have always felt better suited to private corners of the brain. Silversun Pickups completed the return trip we had come for, proving that sometimes nostalgia is not about going backward. Sometimes you just want another round.

The provisions were Peach High Noons, Homestyles, and Garage Beer—a lineup with all the ceremonial dignity a night at Bridgestone requires. Nothing precious, nothing pretending to be dinner at a white tablecloth. Just enough fuel to keep the evening moving from the bands we expected to love toward the one we had badly underestimated.

Then Weezer turned out to be ridiculously fun. Not merely good, not dutifully nostalgic, but the kind of fun that rearranges the story of the night while it is still happening. We came for The Shins and Silversun Pickups. They got us through the door. Weezer made sure the surprise was what followed us home.
