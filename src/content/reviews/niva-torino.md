Then along comes a cup of gelato built on fig and passion fruit, and suddenly the smartest move is not another round of weight but a cold, sharp correction.

The fig gives the order its darker note. It reads ripe, mellow, and a little jammy, the flavor you pick when you want sweetness with some gravity to it. Passion fruit does the opposite job. It brings the acid, the brightness, and the slight tropical snap that keeps the whole thing from turning soft-headed. Together they land in that useful territory where dessert still feels refreshing instead of merely rich.

What helps is that this does not look like fussy gelato pretending to be architecture. It looks like a proper cup: dense folds of fruit-colored gelato, a spoon already in play, and a cone stuck in at the angle of someone who understands that texture matters almost as much as flavor. That little bit of crunch keeps the stop from becoming too polite. It gives the whole thing some contrast and a small sense of ceremony without slowing anything down.

If the point of a seaside dessert stop is to clear the palate and improve the mood in one move, Niva Torino seems to know the assignment perfectly: fig for depth, passion fruit for lift, and just enough style to make restraint feel like a very good decision.
