Central Churrasco looks like the sort of place that has no patience for modern restaurant small talk, and that is very much to its credit. In Matosinhos, the move was a mixed grill, which is really just a polite way of saying the table had decided subtlety could wait for another day. Chicken, ribs, veal chop, bacon, chorizo, and a bowl of rice nearby is not a tasting menu. It is a declaration of appetite.

What makes an order like that work is not novelty but conviction. The chicken comes off the grill with the kind of browned skin that understands fire. The ribs look built for hands rather than etiquette, and the veal chop gives the platter some heft beyond the usual pile-it-high churrasco move. Then the bacon and chorizo push everything in the salt-smoke direction, so the whole table starts to feel less like a meal and more like a minor campaign.

That is also the charm of a room whose official menu is comfortable calling the shot what it is: Misto de Churrasco. No euphemisms, no garnish-as-distraction, no attempt to pretend abundance is embarrassing. The rice matters here because a platter this blunt needs something steady on the side, something to catch juices and calm the salt long enough for you to go back in for another rib or another slice of sausage.

If there is a trade-off, it is that this kind of meal does not leave much room for finesse, and it would be ridiculous to ask it to. Central Churrasco earns its keep by doing the obvious thing with enough smoke, generosity, and confidence that obvious starts to feel like wisdom.
