Casino Espinho has that strange, timeless casino energy. No windows, no clocks, just cards sliding across felt, chips clicking together, and a room full of people quietly convinced the next hand might change everything. Add the minor complication of playing poker in a country where you barely speak the language, and the whole thing becomes considerably more interesting.

The tournament was €95 to enter, 115 players taking their shot. Poker has a wonderfully cruel way of letting you feel clever right up until it doesn't. I survived roughly half the field, finally disappearing somewhere around 62nd. No heroic final table, no triumphant stack of euros. Just the familiar walk away from the table, replaying hands and wondering which decision you'll blame tomorrow.

There was red wine along the way. What red wine? No idea. My Portuguese wine vocabulary extended to "vinho tinto," and frankly, that was enough. Ask for red wine, receive red wine, drink red wine while strangers speak rapidly around you and you try to determine whether the dealer just announced a raise. There are worse ways to spend an evening.

I didn't cash. I didn't come particularly close. But for €95, I got to sit at a poker table in Espinho, drink anonymous Portuguese red wine, and compete with 114 strangers in a game I've loved for years. Sometimes the story you take home is worth more than the money you didn't.
