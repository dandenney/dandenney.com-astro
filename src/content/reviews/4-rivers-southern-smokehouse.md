Airports turn time into something expensive and useless. I was killing some of it at MCO when 4 Rivers changed the assignment. Suddenly there was brisket to order and an old Orlando habit waiting where I had expected another interchangeable airport meal.

When I worked in Orlando, the regular 4 Rivers was part of the landscape. This airport version was not a discovery so much as an unexpected reunion. The order required no strategic thinking: brisket and baked beans, the same smokehouse logic that made sense during those Orlando years and still made sense here. There is comfort in not needing a menu to explain a place back to you.

The brisket is the institution’s calling card, given the long smoke and hand-sliced treatment, while the beans carry brisket and pork into the side dish because restraint has never been barbecue’s governing virtue. A Florida Local Lager completed the airport adaptation—not a grand pairing ceremony, just the sensible local beer beside familiar food while the clock handled itself.

Most airport concessions offer a logo you recognize from somewhere else. This felt different: not a national brand occupying square footage, but a piece of my old Orlando routine turning up precisely when there was time to remember it. Killing time usually means accepting less than you wanted. At 4 Rivers, it meant finding something I had missed without realizing I was looking for it.
