There's something special about hearing an album in its entirety, particularly one that's been part of your life for years. Before streaming turned music into an endless shuffle, albums were little worlds you disappeared into. Hearing Blue October perform Foiled from beginning to end was a chance to step back into one of those worlds. A bucket-list experience, without question.

![Photo of Jess Woodland and her band on stage at The Truth](/no-reserv-ai-tions/blue-october-jess-woodland.webp)

The Truth is a beautiful addition to Nashville's musical landscape, a venue that feels worthy of the city it calls home. Jess Woodland opened the evening with a refreshing humility, a wonderful performance without an ounce of unnecessary theatrics. There's something endearing about an artist who seems genuinely grateful to be standing onstage, sharing something they've made with a room full of strangers.

Then came Blue October. Foiled is an emotionally messy, wonderfully human collection of songs. Addiction, love, regret, desperation, and those fleeting moments when life seems to make sense. Hearing it unfold in its original sequence brought back the emotional architecture of the album, the quieter moments making the heavier ones hit that much harder. And when the final notes of Foiled faded, the band still had plenty of great songs left to give us.

A few Bearded Iris Homestyles, some Busch Lights, and an edible helped soften the edges of reality for the evening. An amusingly democratic selection of intoxicants for a night spent absorbing some rather complicated emotions.

There's a peculiar beauty in revisiting music that once helped you navigate a different version of your life. The songs haven't changed, but the person listening to them has. Twenty years after Foiled was released, standing inside a beautiful new venue in Nashville, we got to experience something familiar in an entirely new way. That's a hell of a gift.
