Ocean Lakes works because it understands that a family vacation with 9 adults and 5 kids is not really a vacation at all until somebody figures out the logistics. This place, just south of Myrtle Beach proper, seems built around that fact. You put everybody in a big house, point them toward the beach every morning, peel off for pool-and-slides duty when the heat or boredom starts winning, and suddenly the whole week has a rhythm sturdy enough to hold a small traveling village together.

The beach is the obvious main event, and it should be. Daily ocean sessions are the kind of habit that keeps a trip from dissolving into everybody freelancing in different directions. But Ocean Lakes has enough secondary infrastructure to keep the place from becoming a one-note sand sermon. The pool, the slides, and the broader Sandy Harbor setup give the kids another lane to burn energy in, while the grown-ups get to pretend that moving from surf to chairs to water park counts as a strategic decision instead of just surrendering to the weather.

Then there are the golf car parades, which sound like exactly the kind of wonderfully specific coastal-campground ritual that makes a place stick in your head after the bags are unpacked at home. A week like this lives or dies on movement: hauling people to the beach, back to the house, over to the next thing, then out again once the evening gets restless. Ocean Lakes seems to know that, and it leans into golf car culture hard enough to make it part of the atmosphere instead of just transportation.

Food and drink followed the right kind of split-screen logic: cooking at the house when feeding fourteen people demanded some control, local buffets when nobody wanted another shift in the kitchen, and beers ranging from Lime Garage to Leffe to Delirium once the day had earned them.
