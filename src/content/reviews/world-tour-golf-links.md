World Tour Golf Links is the kind of place that can make a long absence from golf feel less like lost time and more like a dramatic re-entry. First time back on a course in around twenty years, family vacation in motion, and suddenly Blaine and Caleb are asking if you want in. That is how this one started, and the course turned out to be a pretty good stage for remembering why people let this game colonize so much of their emotional life.

We played best ball, which was exactly the right mercy. It gave the round enough looseness to stay fun while still letting every decent shot feel like a small event. There were a couple of those, enough clean contact to keep the day moving in the right spirit, plus another guy joining along who helped the whole thing feel more like a good outing than some solemn return-to-sport ceremony. That matters when you are shaking rust off by the pound.

The less flattering truth was off the tee. Most of the drives went leaking out to the right, that familiar humiliating shape that makes a golfer start bargaining with physics, posture, and childhood. But even that had a kind of charm to it here. A course built around the grand mythology of famous holes has room for your less-famous mistakes, and sometimes that is all you need: a few good swings, a few ugly ones, and enough open air to let both coexist without ruining the day.

The trade-off is that a place with this much visual theater can tempt you into thinking you are about to play some elegant version of yourself back into existence. Usually you are not. Usually you are just trying to remember how to get the ball airborne without sending it into the next zip code. But if that first round back is supposed to make you want the second one, World Tour Golf Links absolutely did its job.
