The punctuation tells half the story. Knox titled his 2023 EP *I'm So Good At Being Alone?*, question mark and all, then closed it with **I'm So Good At Being Alone**, no question mark. Euphoria hears the EP title as a question and the title track as a statement, a phase of personal reconciliation at the end of an arc that runs from "Man Down" through "Love Letter" to moving on. Knox told the magazine his first record was about losing someone and this one is the process of getting over that. The title track is where he lands, or at least where he says he does.

The first verse sells the perks with a straight face. He wakes whenever he wants, takes both sides of the bed, and skips a party for video games. It's light and a little goofy, and it's honest too, because that small freedom is real. But the lyric keeps leaving its own door open. In the second verse he admits "I won't feel like this forever," then decides to enjoy it while it lasts. That sounds like someone who knows the good mood is borrowed. Then comes "I swear it's not that bad." People don't swear about things that don't need defending.

The section before the last chorus is where the two feelings sit right on top of each other. "There's no one here but I'm not lonely, not lonely," he sings, and every line there doubles its final words, like a person saying something twice to hear whether it holds. He's done crying, he insists. Maybe. The song never settles whether this is healing or a pep talk, and it's better for leaving both in the room.

The sound picks the pep talk. A four-on-the-floor kick drives everything, with hi-hats ticking over a simple, repeating bass line. A synth pad holds underneath while a clean, arpeggiated guitar figure spins on top. It's moderately fast, bright and polished, with enough reverb on the voice and drums to give it room. Knox sings it clear and confident, a little detached, the way you'd tell your friends you're fine. That cool delivery gives him away. The track is dancing while the voice holds something at arm's length.

Knox dropped out of Ohio University, where he'd planned to become a teacher, and moved to Nashville in 2019. He wrote this song with David Charles Fisher, Ben Berger and Ryan Rabin, and Captain Cuts produced it. It sits in Nashville's social-media-driven pop-rock world, and AllMusic compares his music to the folk-pop of Ed Sheeran and the indie rock of The Band Camino. Knox said the song expands on what he and his team were capable of, and the bounce under such a shaky claim backs him up.

It sticks because you can dance to it and doubt it at the same time, and that's what most people getting over someone are doing anyway.
