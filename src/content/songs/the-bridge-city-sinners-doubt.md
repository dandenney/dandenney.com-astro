The upright bass on **Doubt** walks like it has somewhere to be. A plucked line moves under a steady drum kit with a loose swing, a banjo keeps the rhythm going, and a fiddle draws a melody with vibrato over the top. It's a band playing at a dancing tempo, not a funeral pace. That matters, because the song is about the voice in your own head that turns on you.

Libby Lux, the Bridge City Sinners' lead singer, has said the song speaks to the battle within, the sense that you are your own worst enemy. She framed it as a warning against giving in to thoughts of self-harm and self-hatred. That's a heavy thing to carry, and the band doesn't slow down to carry it. The groove keeps pushing forward. That choice reads as its own kind of answer. When the doubt gets loud, you keep your feet moving, keep playing, and stay in the room with the people playing beside you.

The single voice out front is clear and resonant, sung with real expression and no harmonies to lean on. Alone like that, it sounds exposed in a way that suits a song about fighting yourself, because nobody else can step into that argument. The production lets the room in. It's open and natural, raw and unpolished, and the strings sound played rather than sanded smooth.

That rawness has a history. The Sinners started out busking in the Pacific Northwest, and they describe their range as running from Prohibition-era jazz to Appalachian death folk. You can hear a little of both ends here, in the swing of the rhythm section and the fiddle and banjo working together. The band's lineup has Lightnin' Luke on violin, Clyde McGee on banjo, and Scott Michaud on upright bass. Michaud also runs Flail Records, the label the band started themselves, and that do-it-yourself streak fits the sound.

The band says its new album, In the Age of Doubt, follows the direction of 2021's Unholy Hymns, which combined folk instrumentation with heavier, more alternative styles and entered the Billboard charts at number 70. The album returns to the same fictional, hedonistic, post-apocalyptic setting, and this song sits inside it. The logic holds. When everything outside has burned down to excess, the enemy that's left is the one inside. Kris Crummett produced in Portland at Interlace Audio, and he keeps the band close and unvarnished. Every song on the album is credited to the group as a whole, which fits a track that sounds like five people holding a line together.

**Doubt** takes the loneliest fight a person has and sets it to a rhythm you can't help moving to, so you come away feeling that the self-hatred is real, and that you don't have to stand still for it.
