Amy Lee woke up one morning with this arrangement already playing in her head. She went straight upstairs and laid down the synth and a basic vocal. That origin explains a lot about **With or Without You**: it's a version built from whatever arrived first, before anyone could talk it into being bigger.

Bigger is what the original was. U2's 1987 song from *The Joshua Tree*, credited to Bono, The Edge, Larry Mullen Jr. and Adam Clayton, is an arena-rock ballad, a song made for a room full of people to hold up together. Lee keeps the song's push and pull but drops the scale, recasting it as dark, synth-heavy, minimalist industrial rock. Her timing makes the move more interesting. She had just seen U2 at Madison Square Garden in 2015, called the night inspiring, and named this her favorite of their songs. It even made her want to play live again. So the cover is a fan's tribute to a stadium, made in a home studio. The black-and-white video shows exactly that: Lee recording there, and posing by foliage.

The lyric suits the shrinking, because it's about being stuck in a space too small to move in. The speaker waits "on a bed of nails," hands tied, body bruised. The title says it plainly: no way to live with this person, no way to live without them. The most honest line is the one that admits some fault: "You give it all, but I want more." The complaint and the confession sit in the same breath. The speaker knows the other person has given everything and still can't be satisfied, and the song never decides whether that's the lover's failure or the speaker's.

Then comes the phrase that does the real work: "And you give yourself away." It could mean surrender, or betrayal, or simply getting caught, the way a face gives away a secret. The lyric repeats it and then keeps breaking the sentence off after "give," over and over, as if the speaker can't bear to finish it. By the time the speaker has nothing left to lose, there's nothing left to bargain with either. Lee has said of the song: "Love is rarely simple; more often, it's tearing your guts out."

This was the second entry in her YouTube covers series, after Portishead's "It's a Fire." It came the year Evanescence returned from a three-year hiatus. The song later landed on the *Recover, Vol. 1* EP alongside covers of Led Zeppelin and Chris Isaak.

It sticks because it takes a song built for thousands of voices and hands it back to one person in one room, which is where this kind of wanting actually lives.
