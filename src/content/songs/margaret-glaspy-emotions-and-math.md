There's a bath in this song, and it isn't relaxing. On **Emotions and Math**, Margaret Glaspy's narrator spends the nights her partner is away awake until 4 a.m. and the mornings hitting snooze, and she sums up the state in one cold, exact image: "Shiverin in an ice cold bath / Of emotions and math." The longing is there. So is the counting of days, the tally kept because tallying feels like control when nothing else does. Glaspy said the phrase fit the whole record because she was making something emotive that was also process-driven and analytical. Here that tension stops being a working method and becomes the narrator's problem.

What makes the song sting is that she won't let herself off the hook. Things are fine together, she sings, and it's a shame, and she's the one to blame. Then comes the line that carries the most weight: "I'm a woman actin like a kid." It's a confession that is also a scolding. She pictures herself stuck out on a tree limb, a skinny mess left breathless from telling someone everything she's going to do. Big promises, and she knows it. The sharpest turn is the pride she lets slip. She used to be a rolling stone, out on her own, and now that someone's here she lives in fear of them leaving. Independence and dependence sit in the same verse, and neither one wins.

The band plays it with almost no fuss. A clean electric guitar picks out arpeggiated chords, the bass walks underneath, and the drums keep a plain kick, snare and hi-hat pattern at a moderate, easy pace. The room sounds open and natural, and each instrument has its own space. Glaspy's voice is clear with a little breath in it. NPR's Ken Tucker heard an angry bite in that vocal, echoed by a barbed guitar hook. That fits a record he described as having a "deceptive directness": the surface stays calm while the words cut.

Glaspy wrote and produced the song herself. The album was her first full-length, and ATO picked it up after six years of babysitting and playing shows in New York and a plan to put it out on Bandcamp. She re-recorded it at Sear Sound with Chris Morrissey on bass and Tim Kuhl on drums. That history of waiting and stubbornly doing it yourself suits a song about someone who has proved she can stand alone and now hates how badly she wants company.

It sticks because it admits something most love songs dress up: missing someone can make you feel small, and you can still keep counting the days.
