The most deflating line in **Lying To Your Face** is also the funniest. The chorus builds a superhero: if you need saving, the singer will suit up. Then it lands on the question that undoes all of it: "you'll still ask 'why were you late.'" It's the gap between grand devotion and a small, nagging doubt, and anyone who has loved someone who can't quite believe them knows how that sounds across a kitchen.

Evan Honer and Sierra Carson wrote it, and the writing is smarter than a standard he-said, she-said duet. The first verse is a complaint. Everyone the narrator knows would walk away, and still the partner keeps asking if they're leaving. The second verse flips the pronouns and makes the narrator the anxious one. Everyone they've loved has walked away, so now they're the one asking, and they admit it: "it's a pattern I've been trying hard to shake." The accusation in the title turns out to run both ways. Each person is defending against a fear they're also guilty of. The song lets frustration and self-recognition sit next to each other without deciding who's right.

The best image carries that double meaning too: "I'd swim oceans with an anchor round my waist." It sounds like a vow, but it's also a picture of drowning. Love here is a feat of strength performed while something drags you down. The bridge offers the only real way out, a deal rather than a rescue: neither of them goes if the other stays. Then something small happens in the last chorus. The "if" drops away. The earlier hypothetical, that love might not be enough, now comes out flat, as a plain statement.

The sound stays out of the way. A fingerpicked acoustic pattern and a simple, sustained bass line set the floor, with drums built from kick, snare and hi-hat and a ride cymbal on top, all at a relaxed, steady walk. The lead voice is clear and a little nasal, sung like conversation. A second voice harmonizes with it, and two voices sharing the song suits a story where both people carry the same fear. Honer and Shane Travis produced it with a clean, polished finish.

It's one of three new songs added to the deluxe edition of Everything I Wanted, on Cloverdale Records, the artist-run label Honer founded and where Carson is on the roster. Honer has called the album a story about finally giving love a shot, and he says this edition closes that chapter. This song is that story with the hard part left in. Honer is a Nashville-based songwriter who blends folk, country and indie rock, and here the blend stays mostly gentle and acoustic.

What sticks is that line about being late: the song makes you feel how exhausting it is to keep proving yourself, and then quietly admits you're doing the same thing to them.
