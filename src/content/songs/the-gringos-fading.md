The line **"Fading"** keeps coming back to is "I don't want anything to change," and the band plays it like they mean it. The groove holds its shape: a clean electric guitar picks out a looping arpeggio, a bass walks underneath, and the drums settle into a shuffle with the snare up front and the hi-hat ticking along at a moderate pace. A song about someone who can't let go of the way things were rides on a figure that keeps circling back to the same place.

Jacob Reina sings it in a clear, mid-range voice with a slight nasal edge. He sounds relaxed, close to talking. A second voice tucks in alongside him in harmony, and a second guitar adds a melodic line over the top. The production is clean, with some room around everything. That ease is the trick. The words are panicked, but the delivery shrugs, and the gap between the two is where the feeling lives.

The narrator is losing someone and can half see why. "I'm stuck in my ways," he admits in the first verse, repeating what she's been telling him, the same way he repeats her complaint that she's tired of playing his favorite games. Then later the ledger flips: "I've wasted half my life / Just giving you time." He has the self-awareness to know he's the problem and the hurt to insist he's the one who waited. Both are true for him at once, and the song never makes him pick. When he sings that he's running out of precious time, it's hard to tell whether he means the relationship or his own life slipping past while he stands still.

Reina, who shares the writing credit with guitarist Raymond Mann, has said the song is about a relationship gone bad, drawn from general themes rather than one person. That shows in the lyric's plain pronouns and stock situations, and it lets anyone step inside. The Gringos started as a cover band of University of Georgia students playing bars and parties in Athens. They like to call what they do alternative college rock. Reina comes from a country background and Mann is into classic rock. Reina has said "Fading" is probably one of the band's fan favorites live, which makes sense. It's a breakup song you can sway to without noticing how sad it is.

The lyric ends on that refrain sung once more on its own, and it lands like a plea to a room that has already emptied, the sound of wanting to stay exactly where you are while everything moves anyway.
