In **Song for a Girl**, Jared Kolesar hands a woman's confession back to her. Early on she tells him she wishes she were "as interesting / As half the things you read," a small, self-deprecating admission. By the last verse it's his line: "I swear I'm not as interesting as half the things you read." The words barely change. The meaning flips. What was her insecurity becomes his excuse, and the song lives in the gap between the two.

Glide Magazine filed it among the heartfelt breakup tunes on the Orme Dugas EP, set apart from the band's Western barnstormers like "Lost, Scared & Tired." Phoenix New Times went further and called it vulnerable to the point of self-flagellation. That's the right word. Kolesar, who wrote the song, sketches a narrator who knows he's being loved and can't stand it. He calls himself a wanderer, always undone, his soul under shadows, the kind of self-portrait that sounds romantic until you notice it's doing a job. It lets him leave before she can find out anything ordinary about him. The chorus drops the poetry. He admits he's colder than he should be, worn out from running from everyone he's been through, and then he just repeats how tired he is until the phrase sags.

The feelings don't line up, and the song doesn't try to make them. He tells her he's not been higher, then tells her he's nothing she wants. She says she never lets go, and that it keeps her in the past, and he hears it as one more reason he can't be what she's hoping for. He thinks he's protecting her. He might also be protecting himself. Kolesar lets both stand.

The sound keeps that tension from turning into a wallow. Fingerpicked and strummed acoustic guitar carry it, with bass and a plain kick, snare and hi-hat beat underneath, at a moderate tempo. Kolesar's voice is clear with a slight rasp and an earnest, conversational way of delivering even the hard admissions. It's folk-rock in the Lumineers' vein, and fittingly the EP was cut in Nashville with Ryan Hewitt, whose clients include the Lumineers, the Avett Brothers and Brandi Carlile. The production is polished but natural, so the voice stays the center of it.

It closes the EP, the last word on a record Kolesar called the truest representation of the band. That suits a song about someone deciding he's not worth keeping.

The hook is that borrowed line: you hear a person take someone else's tenderest doubt and wear it as armor, and you recognize how often that's what leaving sounds like.
