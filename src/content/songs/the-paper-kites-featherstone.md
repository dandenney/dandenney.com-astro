The kindest line in **Featherstone** is also its saddest: "When you go, what you leave is a work of art." Sam Bentley, who wrote the song and sings it, doesn't describe the mark a departing lover leaves as a bruise or a scar. He calls it art, and he places it on his chest and on his heart. It's a generous way to describe being hurt, and maybe a defensive one. Framing the loss as beautiful lets him keep it without having to say how much it stings.

The song is full of that kind of soft deflection. The lover drifts out into morning haze and gets lost in a tall hedge maze, an image straight out of a storybook. Then the pronouns slip. The song begins by addressing "you," tells of "she" wandering off, then swings back to "you," asking where she went and why she left. In a single verse the distance closes and the questions spill out. The chorus states the hard fact plainly: "And my love is yours but your love's not mine." There's no bitterness in it, just arithmetic. He'll go. They'll meet again down the line. They'll hate what they lost and love what they find.

Then comes the line the whole song leans on: "I'm feeling fine, we've made it to the coastline." It's hard to tell whether he means it. The second time around he adds an "oh" before the claim, a small word that reads like someone talking himself into it. The song never settles whether this is acceptance or a brave face, and it's better for leaving the question open. The ending gives up on words altogether, with the "ooh" refrain repeated seven times, as if whatever is left can't be put into a sentence.

The sound fits that hush. A fingerpicked acoustic guitar rolls through arpeggios at a slow, unhurried pace. Bentley's voice is breathy and close, and the production is clean, with a close room sound. Nothing crowds him. It's Melbourne indie-folk in its gentlest mode, acoustic folk-pop that is more interested in tenderness than in drama. The band had formed around Bentley and Christina Lacy in 2009, and they recorded the Woodland EP in early 2011 with Tim Johnston, one of bassist Sam Rasmussen's university teachers. The Paper Kites are credited as producers.

The song first came out in June 2011, and Nettwerk reissued it in 2013. Along the way it landed in the season 8 finale of *Grey's Anatomy* and found a large audience online, success that Bentley said made him feel very lucky. It's easy to hear why it travels. The song doesn't ask you to grieve loudly.

It hands you a way to say goodbye that sounds like peace, and leaves you wondering, every time that "oh" arrives, whether peace is really what you're feeling.
