"I don't pray to God but I did today." **Feel Something** opens on a confession that undoes itself mid-line, a nonbeliever caught asking anyway. It doesn't feel like faith returning. It feels like someone so worn down that any outlet will do, and the next lines say why: "'Cause I'm scared that I believe in nothin'." The fear isn't of pain. It's of the flatness under it, the suspicion that nothing will answer back.

That's an odd and brave thing to build a bass-music single around. Illenium and Excision made it in the studio after the Global Dance Festival was canceled, Excision said, and they debuted it in September 2019 as the closer of their first back-to-back set at Lost Lands, Excision's own festival. Soundazed heard the split clearly: Illenium's melodic dubstep, Excision's glitch-hop, and I Prevail supplying the voice. It's their second collaboration after "Gold (Stupid Love)," and Heard It Here First called it the darker one, leaning toward an indie-rock hybrid instead of dance-pop. The track sits in the crossover of melodic dubstep and metalcore, Illenium's emotional melodic style set against Excision's aggressive dubstep and rock vocals.

Brian Burkheiser sings the chorus, and the lead voice is clean and clear with a strain at the edges, doubled so it lands as a small chorus of selves. Under it, a sustained synth pad hangs in the air, kick and snare hold a steady, deliberate beat, and the bass stays simple. The production is polished and roomy, and the layers thicken while the voice pushes higher. Eric Vanlerberghe's screamed vocals bring the other half of the argument, the part of the feeling that can't stay melodic.

The verse holds two stances at once. The narrator shrugs at their own blame, half accepted and half resented. Then comes the question of whether to let go, and the admission, "I'm afraid to let go." Release is wanted and dreaded in the same breath, and the song never settles it. The chorus turns that stalemate into a bargain: anything, everything, for one real sensation.

Then the language wears down. Past the chorus the lyric shrinks to a repeated wish just to feel, and to wordless "oh"s, as if words were the first thing numbness takes. In a festival crowd that becomes thousands of people singing vowels together, which may be the closest thing to an answer the song offers: you aren't on your own if everyone around you is aching the same way. It reached number eight on Billboard's Hot Dance/Electronic Songs chart.

The song works because it doesn't ask you to feel better, only to feel at all, and that's a much easier prayer to join in on.
