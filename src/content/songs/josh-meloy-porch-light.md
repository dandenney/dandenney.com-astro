The best line in **Porch Light** isn't in the chorus. It's "We got one more show and it's a shitty door deal," the kind of phrase only someone who has counted out a night's take would think to put in a song. Josh Meloy and co-writer Hunter Dmarcus Baskett fill both verses with that kind of grit: a busted generator, a broken A/C, a morning breakfast that runs seven to nine and gets slept through anyway. The very next line settles the accounts: "Breakin' my back just to make a couple bones." It reads like a ledger more than a lament, and that's why it lands.

The band plays it easy. A fingerpicked acoustic guitar rolls out arpeggios over a walking bass line. The drums keep a steady, unhurried beat with a slight shuffle in it, the kind of groove that sounds like a van holding its lane at night. A steel guitar sits on long, vibrato-tinged notes and slips in fills between the vocal phrases, answering each line like a passenger who's too tired to talk. The vocal is clear and a little raspy, delivered almost conversationally, with no harmonies stacked on top. Nobody is selling anything. The mix is clean and the room sounds natural, polished without turning slick.

That plainness fits where Meloy comes from. He's an Oklahoma songwriter who started writing around 19, living in a camper in Stillwater. That's the same town at the root of the Red Dirt country-rock scene, the world of Cross Canadian Ragweed, Jason Boland and Stoney LaRue. This song doesn't push the form anywhere new. It's a road song in the long country tradition, with a van, a stage, a cheap motel floor and a person waiting at home, and it keeps the details small and true. The refrain comes back again and again, "I'm comin' home, baby, leave the porch light on," and the repetition works like miles piling up.

Rolling Stone called it a word-of-mouth hit with growing crowds behind it, and you can hear why. It's a tired song that never complains, and by the last refrain you'll be leaving a light on for somebody too.
