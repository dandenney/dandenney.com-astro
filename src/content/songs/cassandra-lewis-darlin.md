A walking bass line isn't what you expect under a fingerpicked acoustic guitar. On **Darlin'**, the guitar rolls out its arpeggios like a folk song, patient and close. Underneath, the bass moves step by step, the way it might in a slow-dance country tune or a soul ballad. Cassandra Lewis calls her sound "Cosmic Americana," music rooted in classic country, blues, soul and psychedelic rock, and this pairing fits the name. The country and the soul share one floor, and neither is asked to leave.

The tempo is slow and the groove sways. The drums keep it plain: kick, snare, hi-hat, nothing showy. Over that, Lewis sings in a clear voice with a slight vibrato, holding her notes instead of clipping them. There are no harmonies, and that shapes how the song feels. A title like "Darlin'" is a form of address, and the arrangement respects that by keeping the voice uncrowded. You can hear the room itself, a natural reverb and a clean, polished finish. The band seems to stand a few feet back to give the singer space.

What a listener carries away is tenderness with some weight to it. Sustained notes ask you to wait. A walking bass keeps time like someone who isn't going anywhere. Lewis has said the album this song opens is about her relationship with a man she compares to The Wizard of Oz. The song doesn't need to spell that story out to hold its mood. The steady playing and the unharmonized voice sit side by side, warmth and openness at once, and neither cancels the other.

The backstory adds to its pull. Lewis started writing the record while working on cannabis farms in Mendocino County, and she paid for the sessions with shows and loans. She wrote and arranged every song herself. "Darlin'" came out on its own in May 2021, more than a year before the album. It reached half a million streams and built enough early buzz that she ended up performing for Elektra president Gregg Nadel in the label's parking garage and leaving with a deal. It's fitting that it leads off *Always, All Ways*: the song that opened the door also opens the record.

The live band tracks for the album were cut at Bird & Egg in Richmond, California. The credits for this song list Scott Ferreter on bass guitar and Dan Schwartz on drums, along with Greg Leisz on pedal steel, JP Garau on Hammond organ, Graham Patzner on piano and Chris Hoog on saxophone.

"Darlin'" makes you feel what it's like to be spoken to slowly and on purpose, by someone who has chosen to stay in the room.
