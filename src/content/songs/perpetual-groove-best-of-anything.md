The walking bass on **Best of Anything** keeps moving, and nobody rushes it. Adam Perry's line strolls under a clean electric guitar that picks out chords one note at a time. Matthew McDonald's Rhodes lays down long, sustained chords, and Albert Suttle keeps a steady beat on kick, snare and closed hi-hat. Every part holds its pattern. The four players settle into a moderate, flowing groove and trust it to carry the weight.

Trust is the feeling here, and it seems to belong to the band. Familiar Stare was the first non-single release of new music from the Brock Butler, McDonald, Perry and Suttle lineup since 2007's LIVELOVEDIE. That lineup goes back to a Savannah open-mic night in 2001, when Suttle and McDonald, then active-duty Army members, met Butler and Perry and soon joined up. The songwriting credit on this track goes to all four. You can hear that in how the parts sit together. Arpeggios, walking bass, steady drums and sustained keys each stay in their own lane, and nobody crowds anybody. It sounds like people who already know where the others will be.

The voice is clear and slightly breathy, and it sings melodic lines with a smooth touch. The credits list Perry, Butler and McDonald on vocals, so the singing is shared territory too. The delivery matches the band's patience. It doesn't push or plead. It settles into the groove like one more instrument.

Bandcamp files the EP under jamband, post-rock, indie and electronic rock out of Athens, and you can hear a little of the first two in this track. The jam band part is the sense that the groove matters more than any one player. The post-rock part is the repeated arpeggio, a figure that stays put and lets you lean on it. Perpetual Groove recorded the EP in Athens, at Studio 1093 and Chase Park Transduction, with Tom Lewis producing and mastering. The sound is clean and polished, with a medium-sized room around the instruments and a smooth finish. If you want a jam band to sound loose and ragged, the polish might feel a little too careful. But here the care is part of the point. At nearly seven minutes, the track has room to let its patterns breathe.

**Best of Anything** feels like a long exhale from four people who know each other well, and its steady walk tells you that you can relax too.
