The first line of **1975** sets up two people on different frequencies: "You listen to The 1975 while I just try to feel alive." It's a sly hook. The name-check sounds light, almost like a joke about someone's taste in bands, but the second half of the line is a quiet admission of how hard ordinary living has become. Tash Sultana spends the rest of the song inside that gap: naming the disconnect, insisting on nonfiction over fantasy, then circling toward a night where fantasy might be the only way through.

The pre-chorus is a run of questions, and they read like someone asking permission to feel. Is it weak, is it wrong, does it hurt to cry and let it out. None of them gets answered. The chorus offers escape instead of answers: talking until the lights go out, dancing until the lungs give out, the moon sweeping everyone into galaxies. Then comes the giveaway: "And we convince ourselves for a moment of time." The bliss is real and temporary at once, and the song knows it. That sits close to what Sultana said about the EP's title, that "Sugar" means "synthetic happiness," a name chosen because they were on medication while making the record.

The second verse turns judgment into kinship. The friends getting high, the designer things, the soul sold for a quick fix, and then: "You know we're not so different you and me." The narrator sinks right alongside them. And then comes the line the whole song hangs on: "And my mouth just can not say these things." It's a confession about not being able to confess. Sultana called the track a way of "releasing trauma," revealing "a side of me that I've never spoken about," and also said you're allowed to keep some of you to you. The lyric holds both at once. It promises "someday you will know" and still keeps the door mostly shut.

The sound wears all this lightly. Strummed acoustic guitar and a clean electric line sit over a simple bass part, a steady kick and snare, and closed hi-hats ticking out eighth notes. The tempo is moderate and the groove relaxed, with a polished, clean finish. A single clear voice carries it, mid-range with a slight grain. That plainness fits a song about saying too little.

It also fits how it was made. Sultana wrote the songs for the SUGAR EP. at their own home and studio, and produced, arranged and played every instrument themself. It's a self-contained approach with roots in their Melbourne busking and live-looping days. Here that solitude stops being a technique and becomes the subject.

**1975** lets you sit beside someone who can't say what's wrong, feel the relief of a night that pretends everything is fine, and know the whole time that the pretending is part of the pain.
