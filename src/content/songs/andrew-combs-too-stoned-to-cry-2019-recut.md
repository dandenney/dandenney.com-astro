The cruelest joke in **Too Stoned to Cry** is that the drugs don't kill the pain. They only take away the release. Andrew Combs builds the whole song on that trap. The narrator is lonesome and bled dry, and the one thing that would let any of it out is the thing he's too far gone to do. The last word of the refrain keeps shifting: *cry*, then *fly*, then *die*: "I keep tryin' / But I'm too stoned to die." That's the turn. It's not quite a death wish, and it's not quite a confession. It reads more like a man noticing that even giving up is out of reach.

Combs wrote it for his self-funded 2012 debut, *Worried Man*. Rolling Stone Country pointed to it as one of that record's flashes of brilliance. He has said he loves the song but couldn't stand his original vocal, which sounded "so young and pitchy" to him. So he cut it again for the 2019 deluxe reissue, aiming for something like how his band plays it every night on the road. It's one of his favorite songs to sing live, and the recut has that worn-in ease.

The arrangement stays gentle. A fingerpicked acoustic guitar rolls through arpeggiated chords over a simple bass line. The drums are light, with brushes on the snare, and a clean electric guitar sings a sustained melody against the vocal. The tempo is slow and relaxed, and the room sound is natural and unfussy. Combs sings in a slightly raspy, conversational voice, and he doesn't push. That fits a song this lonesome.

The lineage is plain. Pairing "lonesome" with being too stoned to cry takes the old ache of a lament and numbs it. AllMusic places Combs near Townes Van Zandt and Mickey Newbury. You can hear that kinship in the fatalism without melodrama. The catalog of whiskey, wine, pills, cocaine and easy women is delivered as flatly as a grocery list. The sea imagery carries more of the feeling, in "I'm a sailor who's cold in the worst kind of storm." He writes off salvation in a shrug: they said Jesus could save him, and that was a lie.

What lingers is the gap between the words and the voice. The narrator insists he isn't happy, only numb, yet the band plays it relaxed and unhurried, as if nothing here were an emergency. The song makes you feel grief that someone has sealed off from himself, and you end up doing the crying for him.
