The detail that gives the whole thing away is a cup of tea. Deep into a long list of wishes, Kate Nash sings, "I wish that you knew when I said two sugars, actually I meant three." It's a small, funny line, and it holds the song's whole ache. The narrator doesn't correct the order. She'd rather be misread than look like she wants too much, and she hopes someone will love her closely enough to catch the lie anyway.

**Nicest Thing** is built almost entirely on "I wish." The wishes start sweet and a little shy: being his favourite girl, having her smile be his favourite kind of smile, a beauty spot he loves where no one else can see. Then Nash cuts through her own daydream with one flat word: "Basically, I wish that you loved me." That "basically" is the confession. It's someone getting tired of decorating the thing she's afraid to say plainly.

After that the wishes get darker and more honest. "I wish that without me your heart would break," she sings, and soon she's wishing he couldn't eat, couldn't sleep. There's no cruelty in it, just the lopsided math of a crush. She wants the feeling to go both ways, even if that means wanting him to hurt. Longing and a quiet wish for fairness sit side by side, and the song doesn't pick between them. By the end she's back at the start, still only hoping they might try being something, and no closer to finding out.

The arrangement stays out of the way. An arpeggiated acoustic guitar with a clean, bright tone carries the chords, a walking bass line moves underneath, and brushed snare and kick keep a moderate, slightly swinging pace. The room sounds natural and the production is clean, and Nash's voice sits in front, clear and a little breathy, sung like she's talking to you across a table. There are no harmonies to soften it. She's alone with the list.

Nash wrote it with Jay Malhotra and has called it probably the first song she wrote, the one that made her feel she'd found her voice. She told NPR its subject was simply wanting to be loved by someone. She turned to songwriting while recovering from a broken foot after a drama-school rejection, and she posted her songs on Myspace. That's the same diaristic British pop moment as Lily Allen, who promoted Nash's page, and Nash shares Allen's knack for being funny and crushing in the same breath. Paul Epworth produced alongside her, with the album recorded across London at Eastcote, Miloco and The Pool.

It sticks because it lets you hear what unrequited want really sounds like from the inside: not a grand declaration, but a running list of tiny hopes you'd never dare say out loud.
