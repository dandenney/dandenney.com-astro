The chorus of **Motel Mayday** never changes its mind, but who it's aimed at does. "I should have known better, I should have stayed away," the narrator sings, and he means it. Then in the second verse the line comes back with the pronoun flipped. Now it's the Lord saying it, answering a man who has just picked a fight with heaven over how much he hates the road. That small change is the whole song in miniature: a man trying to file his own bad decisions as somebody else's fault, and being handed them right back.

What pulls him under each time isn't glamour. "She bought me beer and she knew my name," he sings, and the second half of that line does the damage. After the hangover, the fight with his baby and a night slept off by the side of the road, being recognized is the drug. She sings along to every song. For someone who feels this alone, that's enough to follow her anywhere.

Josiah Leming wrote it, and the record's label describes the album's girls, cars, bars and bad decisions as looked back on with humour rather than remorse. There's humour here, sure, starting with Jim Beam introduced as a friend, but the last verse lets the guard down. A girl in Tennessee has a baby that looks like him, so he's headed back up the mountain to send money home. Then comes the swing he can't steady: "Some days I drink til I'm barely alive." Shame and duty sit next to each other and neither one wins. The motel is still waiting when the chorus comes around again.

The band plays it straight, which suits the confession. Strummed acoustic guitar and a clean, arpeggiated electric line ride over a walking bass and a steady kick-snare-hi-hat beat, all at an easy, moderate pace. The vocal is clear and conversational, a little melancholic, with no harmonies to soften it. The room sounds open, and the production is polished without hiding anything.

That polish has a story. The song began as demos in the mountains north of Los Angeles and started the journey toward the album, which Leming finished in London for the British indie Yucatan with producer Duncan Mills, whose credits include Jake Bugg and The Vaccines. So it's an American alt-country road song, cut clean on the other side of the Atlantic.

You keep singing along because the chorus lets you feel exactly what he feels: knowing better, and going anyway.
