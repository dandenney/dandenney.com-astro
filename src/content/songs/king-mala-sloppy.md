The funniest line in **sloppy** is also the one that gives the whole thing away. "God called, she said it's toxic that I like to suffer," KiNG MALA sings, and then, without a beat of reflection: "I blocked her number." It plays as a punchline, but blocking a call means you know exactly what the caller was going to say. The narrator isn't confused about the self-destruction. They've heard the diagnosis and hung up on it.

That's the knot the song keeps pulling at. Under the bravado there's real exhaustion: the narrator played their role, did what they were told, and the universe still has them in a choke hold. So the chorus makes a bargain out of defeat. If life is going to screw them over anyway, they'd rather it be messy, a disaster served on a silver platter, consequences put off until later. Self-blame sits right beside the pleasure. Karma slashing the tires might be deserved, and the fire feels good. The song never decides whether this is freedom or a slow wreck, and it's better for leaving that open.

UPSAHL takes the second verse and pushes the idea somewhere stranger. The future shows up tapping on a window, scary, and gets let in for a bathroom-floor hookup. Then: "She tried to stay over, so I showed her the door." It's a sharp image of someone who'll flirt with tomorrow but won't let it move in. Dread, meanwhile, gets invited straight into bed.

The music refuses to sound beaten. A drum machine thumps four-on-the-floor under a simple, driving bass line, and a bright arpeggiated synth runs over the top. The tempo is quick and built for dancing. The mix is clean, tight and dry, with no reverb to soften the edges, so the lead voice sits right up front: clear, a little breathy, rhythmic and sure of itself. It's a confident delivery of a lyric about having run out of answers, and that gap is where the song lives.

Areli Castro, the artist behind KiNG MALA, is originally from El Paso and now based in Los Angeles. She wrote it with UPSAHL, Jonny Shorr and Rob Auerbach, and the latter two produced. It comes from *honey catching season*, the EP where she says she found the darker, more intense KiNG MALA character after the lighter indie pop of *GEMiNi*. She has framed the record as learning to love every part of herself, and "sloppy" is the part most people would hide. Here, it's the part she puts on the dance floor.

The song lets you shrug at your worst instincts and dance to them, while still giving you that one blocked phone call to admit you know better.
