Near the end of **Clear The Air**, Ryan Young stops explaining and starts bargaining. "Don't leave yet," he sings, promising he hasn't reached the part that makes sense of everything. He never does reach it. The song circles back to its opening, the lines about wanting to tell, wanting to share, the fear that "I'll choke to death if I don't clear the air," and tacks "right now" onto the end. The whole confession turns out to be the run-up to one that still hasn't been said.

That's the feeling Off With Their Heads chose to close In Desolation, their 2010 Epitaph album, with: the panic of saying the hard thing out loud to someone close enough to see the mask. Young admits that the face this person sees from morning to night "is the mask that I put on to hide what's inside," and that it stays on until they fall asleep. The shame comes with real tenderness. He tells them it isn't their fault when he begs and pleads. He worries they'll get scared because they have no frame of reference. Then he asks them to stay anyway and catch him before he falls. He wants to protect them from what's inside him, and he wants them to hold it. The song never picks one.

The bluntest lines are the plainest. He calls himself a failure who will never be significant. He thought getting older would make it go away, and instead it got worse. He says being alone is getting harder, and that's why he has to tell them. There's little to hide behind here except the mask, and the song is the sound of him trying to set it down.

The band doesn't soften any of it. A distorted guitar drives a palm-muted riff at a fast clip, the bass holds to root notes, and the snare hits hard. Young's voice is raw and hoarse, more a melodic shout than singing, with no harmonies to share the load. That's Minneapolis punk, the band's home since it formed in 2002, in the gritty Fest-circuit strain that had their Epitaph debut likened to The Bronx and Against Me!. The confession isn't whispered at a kitchen table. It's hollered over a full band.

The Star Tribune called it the album's epic closer. Jacques Wait produced the album with the band, recorded at the Terrarium in Minneapolis, and some of these songs were first cut for a split with Dillinger Four that never came out. The A.V. Club quoted the mask lines but found the record's production too slick for that kind of feeling. Young has said the lyrics on the album version were very cut and paste. When he recorded it acoustic to open 2016's Won't Be Missed, they decided against punching in, so you can hear him gasping for air.

It sticks because it puts you in the room with someone halfway through the hardest thing they've ever said, pleading with you to stay for an ending they don't have yet.
