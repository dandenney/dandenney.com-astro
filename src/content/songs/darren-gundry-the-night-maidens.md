A plucked acoustic guitar turns over the same arpeggiated figure again and again, like a hand working a spindle, and everything else in **The Night Maidens** settles around it. A sustained pad sits underneath, holding its chords long and still while the guitar keeps circling. Over both, a voice carries a melody. The production is clean, with nothing blurring the line between the picked notes and the held ones.

That contrast drives the song. The guitar is restless and the pad won't hurry. Together they make a stillness that isn't calm, the feeling of standing at the edge of a dark wood at night, sure something is moving out there and unable to see what. The title does the rest. It reads like a name from a storybook's cast of characters, and it doesn't say whether these maidens are a promise or a warning. The arrangement keeps both possibilities open. Wonder and unease share the same room, and neither one wins.

Peter Gundry has worked in this territory since he started composing in late 2013, from a home studio in Queensland, Australia, that he calls Hidden Lore Studios. His music draws on dark fantasy, Celtic music, folklore creatures and neoclassicism. He became widely known through his YouTube channel, and the scene around him includes artists such as Adrian von Ziegler and Brunuhville. Albums like *The Shadow's Bride* and *Salem's Heir* sit in his catalog under his own name.

This one is a family piece. It's credited to Darren Gundry featuring Peter, and the brothers compose together. Peter has described their joint work as installments in a fairytale list they are composing. They had done it before with "A Beautiful Delusion," and they kept going afterward with "The Lurker of Lunar Hollow" in 2024. That context changes how the song lands. Two brothers are building a shared storybook one entry at a time, and **The Night Maidens** plays like a single page from it: a scene set, a presence felt just out of sight. There's a quiet tenderness in that way of working too. The darkness here is something the two of them chose to dream up together.

The song holds you in that suspended moment just before the thing in the dark steps into view, and that's why you keep coming back to it.
