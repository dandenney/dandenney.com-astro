The verses of **This Is Life** read like a junk drawer emptied onto a table: Hollister perfume, drinking in chain-restaurant parking lots, a Brand New singalong from back before Jesse got caught, Montauk summers before it got whitewashed. Nothing gets ranked. Being so broke you did sex shit on Craigslist sits right next to hearing from more famous people that you made it. Then comes the line about parents that hurts most. Once they were security blankets. Now they're wrinkled and racist. The song lets love and disappointment share that single verse and doesn't pick one.

That refusal to sort is the feeling. "Friends from home like old ghosts from past lives" names a kind of grief that isn't quite grief. Nobody died. People just drifted into other versions of themselves. The chorus answers all of it with three words repeated until they stop sounding like a shrug and start sounding like acceptance. To be honest, nine straight "this is life"s can feel thin on the page. Sung, the repetition does what it should. The stacked voices put a main melody under a higher harmony, and the effect is less a hook than a group of people agreeing.

The music stays out of the way of all that confession. A strummed acoustic guitar and a walking bass line carry it at a relaxed, steady medium tempo. The drums keep it simple with kick, snare and hi-hat, and a synth pad holds the chords underneath. The production is clean and polished. The lead voice is clear and a little breathy, so the Craigslist line and the first-time-smoking-at-my-sister's-condo theories on heaven land without any wink.

The song was written by Winnetka Bowling League's Matthew Koma with Medium Build's Nick Carpenter and Dawes' Taylor Goldsmith, and it grew out of fandom. Koma found Medium Build through a viral TikTok song and sent the music to Goldsmith. Carpenter noticed a Dawes poster in Koma's studio and said Dawes was his favorite band, and Koma invited him to meet them at a show the next night. Carpenter has said he didn't believe Koma knew Goldsmith until the free tickets showed up. Koma later told the crowd at the Ryman that he had been to forty Dawes concerts. So when the song announces "Taylor says that we're all gonna die," it plays like a joke among friends, which is exactly what the song says God is: "I think God's a dog out the window," laughing at a joke only friends know. Koma described the collaborators on the acoustic version as "a bunch of sloppy dudes laughing and giddy about singin' in a truck," and that picture of friends sharing a private joke fits the song.

It takes the most embarrassing, tender and ugly pieces of your past and has a room full of friends sing them back to you as if they were simply the shape of being alive.
