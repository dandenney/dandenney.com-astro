"Deep in my structure, I think I still love her." *Structure* is a strange word for a love song. It's more blueprint than heartbeat, and it shows how the narrator of **Demons** sees himself: as something built and load-bearing, now cracking. Later he names a rupture where she should be. Jacob Lee writes like someone trying to reason through a feeling that won't be reasoned with. That fits a songwriter who, asked about influences, named Marcus Aurelius, Paulo Coelho and Mitch Albom rather than any musician.

The betrayal here is specific. The "you" didn't just leave. They switched sides. "But you took their side and you pulled them to freedom" is the chorus's sharp turn. He had been doing the slow work of keeping his worst self locked down, and someone he trusted opened the door. Then comes the line that carries the hurt: "I kept your secrets and I thought that you would do the same." It's a ledger, a fairness argument, and the self-justification sits right next to the wound. He needs it on record that he held up his end. Notice that "her" is spoken about while "you" is spoken to. The song never says whether they're the same person, so grief and accusation stay tangled. Even his requests are small and worn out: sleep, breath, peace.

Lee says he writes everything alone, behind closed doors, on an acoustic guitar, with the band-style production added afterward. Matt Bartlem produced this one. A driving mid-tempo beat, kick and snare hitting square, runs under a sustained synth pad while a distorted electric guitar chugs. Lee's voice is clear and a little breathy, and stacked harmonies thicken the lead. In quieter stretches the drums ease back and the vocal turns close and intimate. Sputnikmusic heard it as mostly pop-rock, with a bridge guitar solo wrapped in swelling synths, and called it a punchy opener for *Philosophy*, his debut.

Lee clearly knew it mattered. He recalls the adrenaline of writing it coming back after about four months of doubt, the sense that he might finally be making something decent. It came out as a single in 2017 and later opened the album, and a re-recorded Philosophical Sessions version followed in 2019. Late in the song the chorus repeats so many times it stops sounding like a complaint and starts sounding like someone saying it over until it makes sense.

What sticks is that last line's quiet bookkeeping, the way you keep counting the secrets you kept when the loyalty never came back.
