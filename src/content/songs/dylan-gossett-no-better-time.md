The strangest bit of advice in **No Better Time** is about crying. "Like the times when you come home, not the times when you're leavin'," Dylan Gossett sings, telling someone to hold their tears for the homecoming and not the departure. Most songs grieve the leaving. This one treats leaving as the easy part, the thing you're supposed to do, and saves the ache for the day you make it back. That reversal tells you whose song this is: someone who stayed too long, or watched someone else do it.

The opening couplet sets two kinds of people next to each other. A quarter in a fountain means somebody still has dreams. A token in a pocket means somebody can't come clean. The song never says which one the narrator is, or which one the "boy" being addressed is about to become. Then comes the line that carries the whole thing: "Sweat on your skin's better than regret on your heart." It's a work-gloves proverb, plain enough to stitch on a pillow. But the next verse shows where it comes from. It describes a face lined by regret, and the pep talk turns into a warning from someone who has paid the bill.

That's the mixed feeling the song won't sort out. It sounds like encouragement, but underneath it is fear, the fear of being old and finding out too late. "You're not livin' unless you're willing to do some dyin' along the way," Gossett sings, and the chorus keeps returning to hands getting weak and a body fading. By the end that chorus has come around three times, closer to pleading than to advice.

The arrangement doesn't push any of it. Acoustic guitar carries the song, a bass holds simple sustained notes, and the drums keep a plain, unhurried beat at a relaxed country-folk pace. A harmonica plays a melodic line over the top. Gossett's low, slightly raspy voice stays conversational, with no harmonies to soften it. The room is small and dry, so it sounds like one person talking across a table. That's the folk side of the Texas country and red dirt Americana he draws on, though he calls himself a country singer from Texas rather than a Texas-country artist.

Gossett wrote it alone and produced the debut EP it anchors, a project he called "very personal." That EP caught the attention of several major labels, and in November 2023 he signed with Big Loud Texas/Mercury. He'd gone from event logistics at Circuit of the Americas to a record deal, which looks a lot like taking his own advice.

The song works because it's pushing you out the door while it's afraid of what happens if you don't go, and you can hear both feelings in the same voice.
