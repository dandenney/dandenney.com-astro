A patch of sunlight on a carpet is a small thing to build a song around, and that smallness is the point. **Never Gonna Be Alone** opens with the narrator looking at the spot "on the carpet where I held you for a moment in June," and everything after is an effort to stay inside that moment. Jacob Collier wrote it with his friend Lizzy McAlpine in the depths of lockdown, and he has said he wanted to explore isolation, loss and memory. You can hear all three in where the lyric goes: a room, a window, a door, a garden at night with a tree looking up at the moon. Nobody leaves home. The person being loved exists only as a place.

The chorus is where the feeling splits. "You'll be right where I left you, sitting on the floor" sounds like comfort, a lover waiting and unchanged. But only a memory can stay that still. So when the line turns to the title, the promise of never being alone is also an admission that the real company is gone. The stretch that breaks the pattern says so outright, "Even though I know / Nothing's gonna change," then swerves straight back to devotion, vowing to keep finding the way back. The song never settles whether that return is healing or a trap. It holds gratitude and grief in the same hand.

The sound matches that hush. Acoustic guitar rolls through arpeggios, a bass walks underneath, and brushed drums keep a slow, relaxed pace. The room is spacious, the reverb noticeable, the whole thing warm and polished. Collier and McAlpine trade verses, and on the chorus their voices intermingle, moving between unison and close harmony so you can't always tell where one ends. For a musician whose style grew out of stacking his own voice into dense, genre-blending harmony, this is a close, intimate setting: two singers, one memory.

John Mayer's guitar threads through it. The electric plays a sustained melody that works like a third voice. Collier called having Mayer lend his tones a surreal thrill, and said he grew up listening to Mayer's music in a deep way. The trio first played the song together in October 2022 at McAlpine's Troubadour show, and the track later found a home on Djesse Vol. 4.

The last lines circle back to the sunlight and the carpet and stop on the moment itself, with June left off, the memory cut short before it can finish. The song teaches you how loneliness can disguise itself as company, and lets you feel how tender and how sad that disguise is at the same time.
