The prayer at the center of **False Idols** arrives in a hook whose meaning nearly slips past: "Makes you hurt again / So you can heal and say amen." That is abuse dressed as worship, a god who wounds you so the comfort feels earned. Suzanna Son sings the hook, and the song lives in the space between that hymn and the rap around it, where people with every reason to feel secure keep admitting they don't.

The verses are a ledger. Contracts with "baseball numbers," eight-figure taxes, a million for an hour-long set, diamonds that "really be diamonds." The bragging is real, but it keeps snagging on something. "But I went through a lot for this money and fame" turns the flex into a receipt for damage. Then the narrator mentions a big brother at the end of his habeas, says he talks to him daily and has told him he's still coming home. The very next line frets that the brother will take it as empty talk. Pride and guilt sit in the same bar, and neither wins. Later the paradise boasts collapse into "But these false idols got me terrified," and the fear stops sounding like a pose.

The production keeps all this cool on the surface. A low synth pad hangs under everything, fast hi-hats tick steadily, and a prominent kick and snare keep time. The groove is laid-back and mid-tempo, unhurried even when the words are frightened. The rap is mixed dry and close, clear with a slightly nasal edge, confident and conversational, with no harmonies padding it out. That intimacy matters. The fear arrives at speaking volume, as if nobody's supposed to overhear.

Abel Tesfaye wrote it with Mike Dean, Son, Dominique Jones (Lil Baby) and Jordan Holt-May. Dean produced, mixed and mastered it, with The Weeknd also producing. It's the second time The Weeknd and Lil Baby have worked together, after "Hurricane" on Kanye West's 2021 album *Donda*. The song was written for HBO's *The Idol*. In episode five, Son's character Chloe performs it for Finklestein, Chaim and Nikki as they wait for Joss to arrive. Heard that way, the closing chant about a "he" who will find her and fix her stops being devotional and starts sounding like a threat. Lines about walking with the "cameras off" and "I'm not an actor" blur the show into the song. The track came out the same day as The Weeknd's solo "Like a God," a phrase the hook here keeps circling.

The song makes you hear wealth as a wall around fear, and its hook is the one asking you to thank whoever hurt you.
