The best line in **Might Be Right** is an admission of failure set to music that refuses to sound defeated. "And I always fall / A little short in front of you," Tony Esposito sings, and the band loops it, a self-own repeated until it turns into a chant. Nobody in the song is wallowing. They're just saying it louder.

The engine is a distorted electric guitar riff that drives hard and brisk, locked to a tight, punchy drum beat with the snare out front. The bass sits underneath and keeps things solid. Esposito's voice is clear and mid-range with a raw edge, and a second voice doubles him in unison or stacks a close harmony on top. It sounds like a band playing together in a room. That fits what Esposito said about making the album: it felt easy because the group already knew the songs and played most of them live, like a practice.

White Reaper came up in Louisville, and their sound has always leaned on garage-rock crunch and power-pop hooks. On *You Deserve Love*, they added some '80s AOR and glam to the mix. This is the lead single from that record, their Elektra debut, made in Nashville with producer Jay Joyce, whose credits include Cage the Elephant and Eric Church. You can hear a big-room rock record here, but the songwriting stays scruffy and close.

The lyrics are a breakup told in odd, particular details. A shirt tied into a bow. A stash someone stole. A sister who gets a told-you-so. Then the narrator turns on himself: "I may have tricked myself, she couldn't shake it / So I guess you might be right." It's a grudging concession, delivered with a shrug and a big chorus. Even the mother's warning, that when things are getting bad they might be getting worse, gets tossed off like a thing everybody already knew.

Then the song strips down to a mantra, "Keep the lights down," repeated until it works like a dare. The record made White Reaper's first Billboard chart appearance and later reached No. 1 on the Alternative Airplay chart.

You come away feeling like you've lost the argument and decided to sing along anyway.
