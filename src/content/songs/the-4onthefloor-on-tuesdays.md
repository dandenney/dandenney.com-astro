On Bandcamp, the 4onthefloor give this song one more word: **"(Drunk) On Tuesdays."** The streaming title drops it, and on its own "On Tuesdays" reads like a standing appointment. Put the word back and it becomes an admission about the least festive night of the week. Nobody gets drunk on a Tuesday for a holiday. That small edit is the emotional key to the track.

Gabe Douglas, who sings and plays guitar in the band, sits in the middle of his range with a raw, unpolished edge. Nothing is smoothed over. In places a second voice comes in underneath with a lower harmony, and it shifts the mood. A weeknight drunk is lonelier sung alone. The harmony sounds like someone on the next stool keeping pace.

Under them the groove moves at a moderate, driving clip. The drums hold a steady beat with a firm backbeat. The bass keeps a simple, repeating line that never reaches for attention. An electric guitar picks a clean arpeggiated figure, then digs into distorted, rhythmic strumming, and the band leans harder as it does. It plays less like a release than a shrug turning into a stomp.

That stomp is the band's whole idea. The 4onthefloor write every song in 4/4, and each of the four members plays a kick drum alongside his own instrument. The name comes from it, and so does the album title, *4x4*. Asked how hard the rule makes songwriting, the band put it at about a four out of ten, which sounds like a joke and a fact at the same time.

*4x4* was their debut, cut quickly at Creation Audio in Minneapolis, the first time all four had been in a studio together. The sound carries that hurry. It is dry, close-miked and energetic, with no polish to hide behind.

The tradition here is bar-room blues rock, the line critics draw through Creedence Clearwater Revival, the North Mississippi Allstars and the Black Keys. The song's economy recalls Creedence, and it is over in under two and a half minutes. The close, scuffed recording sits near the Keys. Then it builds all of that on the band's own rule: four players, four kick drums, four beats to the bar.

Douglas is credited as writer on live recordings of the song from Codfish Hollow in 2019 and First Avenue in 2021, so it stayed in the set long after the debut.

It makes an ordinary weeknight feel worth stomping your foot for, without pretending it was ever anything more than an ordinary weeknight.
