Take a metal classic down to fingerpicked guitar and bass and what's left of **The Number of the Beast** is a nightmare told by someone too shaken to shout. Shawn James & the Shapeshifters play Steve Harris's 1982 Iron Maiden song as a slow ballad. A fingerpicked acoustic guitar rolls out arpeggios over a low, sustained bass line. Shawn James sings in a clear, resonant voice, smooth and drawn out, with no harmonies to share the weight. The room is open and lightly reverbed, the texture clean. The Moshville "Classic Covers" column called the band's sound very laid back rock music, and that's the shock of it: a song about hell and sacrifice delivered like a confession at closing time.

The quiet makes you hear what the lyric was always about. Before any torches or chants, the narrator is alone and unsure, trying to "get the memories from my mind" and asking whether what they saw was real. The most frightening image isn't the ritual at all. It's this: "Was a reflection of my warped mind / Staring back at me." The fear points inward. At this tempo the doubt sits in the open, and the horror reads as a person who can't trust their own head.

Then the voice changes hands. Late in the lyric, the speaker stops being the witness and becomes the thing itself: "I'm coming back and I will return." The narrator's dread and the beast's promise come out of one voice, and the song never says where one ends and the other begins. The chorus repeats "six, six, six" over and over, and against a smooth, unhurried delivery it plays less like a battle cry than a phrase someone can't stop saying.

The reading fits the record around it. *The Covers* puts this next to "John the Revelator" and "God's Gonna Cut You Down," titles that live in the same world of judgment and reckoning. James, a Chicago songwriter, has said he's fairly obsessed with mythology, sagas and legends. That shows here: he treats Harris's pulp imagery as folklore, something told low by the fire. The album was recorded live by Nick Futch, who also played drums, with Phillip Farris on bass and violin, banjo, harmonica and mandolin in the wider lineup. The band took this one to a stage in Germany in 2014, alongside Motörhead's "Ace of Spades."

Slowed down and sung softly, the song stops being a spectacle about evil and becomes the private terror of seeing your own face in the dark and not being sure it's yours.
