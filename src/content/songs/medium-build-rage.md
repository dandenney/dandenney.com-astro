The word **"Rage"** never gets shouted. Medium Build sings it in a breathy, slightly nasal voice with a little vibrato on the held notes, over clean electric guitar arpeggios and a slow beat that drags its feet. The music doesn't sound like rage at all. That gap between the title and the sound is the song.

What the lyrics describe isn't an outburst. It's an admission made by someone who wants to be held. The opening lines ask to be kissed, held, and squeezed close. The narrator says they've spent a long time trying to act tough, and then comes the request that cuts deepest: "Say my real name." This person has been living as some armored version of themselves, and the plea is to be seen past it. Carpenter has said he started Medium Build as a safe place for his intrusive thoughts, and that the persona eventually merged with who he really is. The song doesn't explain the line, but those two ideas sit close together.

The chorus repeats one confession, the rage inside, and adds nothing to it. No reason, no target, no apology. The admission comes later, in the verse where the song stops circling: "I get scared, I get mean." Six plain words connect fear to cruelty, which is the most honest thing a person in a fight can say and the hardest to say out loud. Right before that comes regret buried in the chest, and right after it a request not to take the love away. The narrator doesn't ask to be let off. They ask to be called out, "Please don't give up on me now," which means asking for accountability and patience at once. Shame and need sit side by side, and the song leaves them there.

The band plays like it's trying not to startle anyone. The bass holds simple root notes. The drums keep a steady kick, snare, and hi-hat. The guitar picks through clean arpeggios with a little reverb around them. The production is polished and roomy, and there are no harmonies to soften the voice. Carpenter wrote it, Laiko produced, and the song later closed Medium Build's *Health* EP on Island. That's a fitting place for it: an ending that is really a request to stay.

The song lets you feel how anger can be the loudest form of being afraid you'll be left, and it makes the quiet version of that fear sound like the true one.
