The cruelest image in **One More Shot** is a white dress. "You dressed me in white / To just lay me down," Cil sings, and the line folds a bride and a body into one picture. The white was supposed to mean forever, and it became a shroud. The rest of the song grows out of that image. She says she would have stayed until death took her, in the language of a vow, and then he handed her the gun himself.

The title carries two meanings. One more shot is a second chance and a bullet, and the song never chooses between them. Cil wrote it with Annika Wells and Dan Gleyzer, and the writing keeps both meanings tangled through every chorus. She offers forgiveness in the same breath that she describes his body going cold. The song opens on a claim of victory and a gloat that he must regret things now. But a later verse shows how long the hurt has been building. She used to hold him through the night, until she noticed the purple under his eyes, the drugs he's still doing, the girl he calls just a friend, the bruise on his neck. "Oh, I'm not surprised," she sings. It's the saddest line in the song, because being surprised would mean she still expected better.

Then the bridge breaks the act. Whether she kills him depends on whether he's sorry. The revenge fantasy turns out to be a plea, and the menace is how a person who's been lied to keeps from begging. Listen to the chorus shift between rounds. The first time, she accuses him of wanting to leave. The second time, she tells him to go if he wants to. The rage hasn't cooled, but that permission is the closest she comes to letting him go.

Gleyzer produced the track, programmed it and played every instrument, and he keeps it lean. A steady beat with a prominent snare sits over a bass guitar, with piano chords on top and a synth pad filling in underneath. Yianni Anastos-Prastacos mixed it, and the sound is dry and clear, so nothing stands between you and the voice. Cil's tone is mid-range with a slight rasp, and that grain makes the threats sound like confessions. It's soul-pop in the line of the singers she names as influences, Amy Winehouse, Etta James and Lana Del Rey: a soul singer's voice carrying a pop track built from programming.

This was her debut single, released about a year and a half after she moved to Los Angeles alone at 18. It was gaining traction on TikTok and has passed 60 million streams. It was the only song she had out when she played her first show, and she still counts on performing it at every one.

It sticks because it lets you say the vicious thing out loud and still hear, underneath, how badly she wanted him to stay.
