**One More Shot** starts with a victory lap. The narrator announces a win before telling us what the fight was, and by the time the details arrive, the win looks a lot like a wound. The song is built on one pun. A "shot" is a bullet, and a shot is a second chance. The chorus keeps both loaded at once, so every promise of forgiveness lands as a threat, and every threat sounds like someone begging to be asked back.

The image that sets it up comes early: "You dressed me in white." The next line finishes it as a burial instead of a wedding. Bridal white becomes the clothes you lay a body out in. The narrator would have stayed until the end. Instead the partner hands over a gun. That gift is the betrayal itself, because it gives the narrator the power to punish. The rage is real, but so is the bitterness of having been given no choice but to feel it.

The second verse is where the song stops posing. Before the cheating there was tenderness: holding someone all night, then noticing the purple under their eyes. The narrator clocks the drug use first, then the lie about "that girl," then the bruise on the neck. The detail that cuts deepest is "I'm not surprised." That isn't fury. It's exhaustion, the sound of someone who saw it coming and loved them anyway. Then comes the line that gives the whole act away: "Well that just depends if you're sorry." The killer wants an apology. The chorus declares the partner already dead, and in the next breath offers one more shot. Pride and longing never settle. When the chorus returns, it ties the same knot, with the narrator promising to put the partner to rest, a phrase that could mean a funeral or finally letting go.

Cil wrote it with Annika Wells and Dan Gleyzer (Kaizoku). It came out of a freestyle session, and the lyric has that loose, talked-out feel, with scraps of argument stitched into hooks. Bēkon, Chakra and Pete G produced it, with Chakra on guitar and Bēkon on mellotron. It was her debut single, released on Warner a year and a half after she moved to Los Angeles alone at 18. It caught on through TikTok and became the only song she had out when she started touring. She names it as a guaranteed part of every show. Her influences run through Stevie Nicks, Etta James, Amy Winehouse and Lana Del Rey, and the song carries that line's taste for heartbreak dressed up as danger.

It sticks because you can hear the bluff and still believe the hurt, and every time the chorus comes back you're waiting to see whether the narrator pulls the trigger or opens the door.
