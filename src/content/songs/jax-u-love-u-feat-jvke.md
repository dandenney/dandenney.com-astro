"I'm not saying that we're narcissists," goes one of the first lines of **u love u**, and the joke lives in the hedge. Nobody says that unless the thought has already crossed their mind. Jax has said the song started in a studio session where a group of friends set out to write a self-love song and "the cockiest love song ever." The cockiness is real, but so is the nervous little throat-clear that comes before it.

The idea is simple and very funny. Two people fall for each other because each one is already in love with themselves. The chorus spells it out, "You love you, I love me," and the line lands like a contract, two separate satisfactions running side by side. Jax has described the serious version: it's easier to start a relationship when you're secure on your own first. The song wears that wisdom as a smirk. It shrugs off drama, brags that past lovers never measured up, and keeps saying how unbothered the pair is.

Then the second verse lets something slip. "Yeah, you get me high / When I'm low, low, low." For a song built on not needing anyone, that's an admission. The low exists, and the other person is the one who lifts it. The chorus says the pair isn't stressed about their insecurities, which still means there are insecurities to not stress about. Bravado and need sit in the same verse, and the song never sorts them out. That's what keeps it from being only a bit.

The sound stays out of the way. A synth bass holds long notes under a steady drum machine pattern of kick, snare, and hi-hat, with a synth pad stretching sustained chords overhead. The tempo is moderate and the groove is plain pop, dry and polished. The lead vocal is clear and direct, delivering the boasts with a straight face, and the echoed answers written into the lyric in parentheses give the hook a call-and-response bounce.

The melody borrows from a well-known nursery rhyme, which fits the TikTok-parody instincts Jax built her following on. A tune everyone half-knows makes the lyric land at once. Producer Jason Hahs co-wrote it, and JVKE sings on it, a pairing that came after Jax followed his work on TikTok. Jax came to this by an unusual road: a throat cancer diagnosis at 18 left her unable to sing, and she turned to writing songs.

At just over two minutes, it's a schoolyard melody carrying grown-up armor, and what sticks is the crack in that armor, the moment you hear someone insist they're fine on their own while admitting the other person is the one who picks them up.
