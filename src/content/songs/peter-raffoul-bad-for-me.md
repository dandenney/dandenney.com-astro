The phrase that matters most in **Bad For Me** is the one that doesn't finish. Again and again the chorus reaches for its reason, "to make me feel better," and on the fourth pass it stops short: "To make me feel." The word "better" just falls away. It's a small cut, but it changes the whole confession. The narrator isn't chasing happiness anymore. They're chasing any feeling at all, and the song keeps landing on that gap until it ends there, still unfinished.

Peter Raffoul built the song from a plain idea he's described. When you're going through a breakup or some other hard stretch, doing what's bad for you can feel easier, because it numbs you. The verses spell out the trap without excuses: weeks of getting high and staying numb, places you shouldn't be, and the flat admission that "something / Always seems to feel like nothing." That line holds the real ache. The trouble isn't that nothing helps. It's that even the things that should help feel like nothing.

Later the song names what the numbness is covering. The narrator pictures someone else holding an ex and telling them things the narrator once said, then asks the question underneath everything: "Or am I in this alone." Self-destruction sits right next to a stubborn, almost hopeful need to know the other person hurts too. The song doesn't pretend that need is noble, and it doesn't settle it either.

The sound stays gentle where a lesser song might lean on drama. A fingerpicked acoustic guitar rolls through arpeggios. Raffoul has said co-writer Connor Thuotte played a simple chord progression on a $5 yard-sale guitar, and that guitar part ended up on the final version. Bass holds sustained root notes, and a slow kick and snare keep the beat without hurry. Raffoul's voice is mid-range with a little rasp, sustained, with a light vibrato, sometimes shadowed by a softer harmony above. Owen Lewis's production is clean and dry, so nothing blurs the words.

Raffoul, a Leamington, Ontario native working out of Nashville, wrote it with Thuotte and Kate York on his debut EP, *Songs from House In Blue*. It fits the confessional, soul-leaning balladry he draws on, and his own description of his songs, admitting things are far from perfect and finding a kind of hope in saying so. The hope here is in the honesty itself.

That clipped "to make me feel" is the hook that stays with you, because it catches the exact moment when wanting to feel better gives way to just wanting to feel anything.
