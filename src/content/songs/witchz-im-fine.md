The prayer in **Im Fine** doesn't get far. Witchz sings "I just said a prayer," then gives it: "God help me," four times, the same three words. Then the order flips and the plea turns into an admission, "I'm fucked up all the time." That's the song's emotional truth in miniature: someone reaching for help in the only words they have left, and finding those words wear thin after a few repetitions. A few lines later comes the doubt about whether the world is an evil simulation or the problem is just their own mind, which is what exhaustion sounds like when it starts looking for an explanation.

The first verse goes from not wanting to wake up to feeling "kinda vile," then lands on a shrug: "But hey I been here before." That line is half comfort and half warning. Familiarity is supposed to make despair manageable, and here it mostly makes it routine. The narrator isn't surprised by the low. They're tired of it.

The chorus is where the hiding starts. It stacks short lines. They can't swallow, they insist this isn't like them, they know people don't like them, and they offer to leave. Then: "Save our time / I'm fine." The leaving gets framed as a favor to everyone else, and "I'm fine" lands as the most obvious lie in the song, the thing you say to end the conversation before anyone looks closer. The second chorus changes one word. "This ain't like me" becomes "they ain't like me," and the denial about the self turns into distance from the people around them. It's the same isolation from the other side.

Witchz is a Las Vegas producer and songwriter who makes all the music and handles their own promotion. They came up through dark, breathy covers of rock and pop classics that spread on TikTok, among them Nirvana's "Smells Like Teen Spirit," Kate Bush's "Running Up That Hill" and Chris Isaak's "Wicked Game." The "Teen Spirit" cover arrived about three weeks before this single, and the two now sit side by side among the artist's top songs on Apple Music. Michael Lattino is credited as composer, and at just over two minutes the song is sharper for its brevity.

Witchz has said they looked for a singer until about a month before releasing their first music, and only started singing after seeing the Bradley Cooper/Lady Gaga film. There's something fitting in a record this private coming from someone who stepped up to the microphone when no one else was there.

It sticks because you recognize the reflex it catches, saying you're fine at the exact moment you are furthest from it, and hoping someone hears the gap.
