"Couldn't look you in the eye." That's the first admission in **Creep**, and Arlo Parks sings it as if she still can't. Her voice is breathy and soft, pitched close enough to feel like it's meant for one person, over arpeggiated acoustic guitar and a simple, sustained bass line. The tempo is slow, with a slight swing that keeps it moving like breathing. There are no harmonies to hide behind and nothing in the mix to blur the edges. It's clean, polished, and quietly exposed.

Parks said she wanted to strip Radiohead's song down to its core, and you can hear what that core is once the alt-rock armor comes off. This is a song about shame that keeps tipping into want. The narrator puts the other person on a pedestal, calling them an angel, and then turns the knife inward. Listen to how the hook flips between verses. At first the narrator wishes to be special and then tells the other person they are. Later the same two lines come back in reverse order, the envy and the worship trading places, neither one winning. The most painful moment might be the smallest: "I want you to notice / When I'm not around." It's the wish of someone who can only imagine being seen by disappearing. Parks doesn't push it. She lets it sit, and the restraint makes it sound less like an outburst and more like something said late at night to a friend.

Then the song does something strange. It steps outside itself. For a few lines the narrator stops talking to anyone and just watches someone run out the door, the words breaking down into a repeated "she runs." The song itself seems to bolt, out of the room and out of the feeling, until a last round of "whatever makes you happy" and the chorus pull it back to the only thing it has left to say: the narrator doesn't belong here.

The context makes the reading hard to miss. Parks recorded the cover at Air-Edel Studio 1 with engineer Nick Taylor for *Shy Radicals*, Tom Dream's short film about Hamja Ahsan and his satirical manifesto for shy and introverted people. She first covered it that April, in a live-stream hosted by the mental health charity CALM, where she is an ambassador. It followed "Black Dog," a song written to make people struggling with their mental health feel less isolated. She has called "Creep" both delicate and brutal, a refuge in low moods, and the first Radiohead song she ever heard from the band she loves most. This version sounds like that refuge, a fan returning the favor to a song that once kept her company.

Sung this softly, the self-loathing stops sounding like a pose and starts sounding like a confession you'd want to answer, and that's why it stays with you: it makes you want to tell the person singing that they belong.
