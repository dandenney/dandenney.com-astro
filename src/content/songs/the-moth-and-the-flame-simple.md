The strangest line in **Simple** is the one that sounds like comfort. "There is no way you can understand," Brandon Robbins sings, and then he offers love as a guide anyway, and a promise that things will work out in the end. It's a love song built on a contradiction it never tries to fix. The narrator insists everything has "always been so simple" while telling the other person they couldn't possibly follow it. That's how certainty sounds when it's trying to calm someone else's doubt. The singer is sure, but the reassurance gives away that somebody in this room isn't.

The opening sets that up. The world says to wait and see, and the narrator answers, "But I've known since the day I fell." There's pride in that line, a quiet flex of conviction. But the song keeps circling back to persuasion, which suggests the certainty still needs defending. Then, when the second stanza comes around again, something small and telling happens: the invitation to be led by love ends at "Let love take you by," and the hand never arrives. Whether you hear that as a stumble or as the moment words stop being enough, the gap matters.

The sound leans toward warmth over drama. A clean electric guitar plays shimmering, sustained arpeggios. Bass holds long root notes underneath, and the drums keep a simple kick, snare and cymbal pulse at a slow, gently flowing tempo. Robbins' voice is breathy and a little strained, as if the plainness of the lyric costs him something to say out loud. A higher harmony voice joins him and fills in the space. The mix is polished and roomy, with plenty of air around everything.

The band formed in Provo, Utah, in 2011, the same scene that produced Imagine Dragons, and it's now based in Los Angeles. Drummer Andrew Tolman played in that band before this one. Tolman, Robbins and keyboardist Mark Garbett share the writing credit. The single arrived on Robot Farming on December 19, 2016, less than a year after the album *Young & Unafraid*. The next day, Robbins had a kidney transplant from his longtime friend Corey Fox, and the band went on hiatus while they recovered. The lyric never mentions any of that. It just keeps repeating that things will work out.

The song holds out its steadiness like a hand, and you feel how much the person offering it needs you to take it.
