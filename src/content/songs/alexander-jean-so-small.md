A guitar riff can wait a long time for its reason. Alexander Jean say **"So Small"** started as exactly that, a riff and an idea they set aside for the day they had a baby of their own. In 2023 that day came into view. Mark Ballas and BC Jean, married since 2016 and working as a duo since 2015, knew their first child was on the way, and they finally wrote the song. It came out on November 2, 2023. Their son, Banksi Wylde Ballas, was born three days later. Release dates rarely matter much, but this one does. The song arrived just before the person it was written for.

The riff is a fingerpicked acoustic pattern, arpeggiated and unhurried, the kind of figure that rocks rather than drives. The tempo is slow, and the whole thing moves with the gentle sway of a ballad. The lead voice is breathy and close, holding notes long and smooth instead of clipping them. A second voice joins in harmony above it, adding lift without crowding the first. The production is clean and polished, set in a room with enough natural reverb that the guitar and voices have air around them without drifting away.

That restraint fits the brief the duo gave themselves. They call it a lullaby for anyone who is a parent or about to become one, and they describe it as a musical diary of the chaos and transformation of that change. What's moving is that the two descriptions pull against each other. A lullaby is meant to calm someone. A diary of chaos admits there's plenty to calm down about. The song sits in the space before the birth, when the fear and the tenderness haven't sorted themselves out yet. It sounds like two people trying to soothe a child who isn't here, and maybe soothing each other in the process.

The title holds wonder and disbelief at once. Something that will reorder an entire life, and it's small enough to hold. The duo don't oversell it. Coming at the end of a busy 2023 that also brought "Sounds Like Fun," "I Love It" and "Rainbow," this one is the quiet, acoustic-leaning turn in their catalog.

The honest catch is that the polish takes the edge off the chaos they describe. The record is so smooth that the upheaval comes through more as an idea than as something you hear. Some listeners will want a little more rough grain in a song about life being turned over.

Still, it works as a lullaby should, slowly and kindly, and you're left inside the hush of two people waiting by the door for someone they've already started singing to.
