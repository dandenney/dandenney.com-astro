**John Wayne** opens with Buzzy Lee's voice, singing a few lines that settle on a bare admission of how hard it's been, then break off mid-comparison. Denzel Curry's hook finishes the thought: he walks around the hood like John Wayne. The name becomes a costume a Black man wears to survive the actual law.

The song is about the police, and Curry has said so plainly. He told Loud and Quiet he feels he has to carry a weapon to protect himself from the people who are supposed to protect him. That fear has a source: his brother Treon "Lotto Tree" Johnson died after being tased by police in 2014. Hear the verses with that in mind and the bravado shows its seams. "911, emergency will murder me the day I call 'em" is a threat on the surface and terror underneath. When he admits "So much pain I've endured, now I'm painless," the boast is really numbness, a scar he has decided to call armor.

The chorus holds the knot that never comes loose. "I'ma give them what they gave us on the first grade" turns revenge into something old and childish, a debt that started in grade school and never stopped compounding. It's pride and grief at once. Later he says his little weapon gave him power when he aimed it, which is as honest as the song gets about what the gun means. It's less protection than the first time he felt anything but small. He even hedges in the middle of a kill-or-be-killed line with a shrugging "I guess," and that small word carries more doubt than any of the swagger around it.

JPEGMAFIA, one of the few collaborators Curry stayed close to through the pandemic, produced the track. It's dry and close: a drum machine with a hard snare, a bass pattern that repeats without relief, and high synths hanging above it all. Curry raps fast and sure on top, with nothing to blur his edges. The cleanliness is the point. There's no room sound to hide in. The track also carries an echo of the video for the album's lead single "Walkin," which ends with Curry facing an actor credited as "John Wayne."

This is the Carol City kid who came up through SpaceGhostPurrp's Raider Klan, now aiming his old underground aggression at a larger target. The hook keeps saying "we sing," turning one man's armed vigil into a chant for everyone in the struggle. The lyrics close on a curse aimed at someone who's already lying.

It sticks because the chorus makes you shout along with a revenge fantasy before you notice it's really a confession of fear.
