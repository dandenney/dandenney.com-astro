Close your eyes facing a sunset and you know the color Nai Palm is singing about. "It feels like I'm inside my eyelids" is the best line on **Red Room**. It turns a lit-up bedroom into something you feel from the inside: warm, blood-tinted, private. A line earlier, she's inside a flower. The images keep folding inward, from the room to the petals to the skin over your own eyes. The song isn't looking at a beautiful thing. It's sealed up inside one.

Nai Palm said the idea came from the red light in her vocal booth and from an old house full of red glass, where her whole room went red at sundown. The lyric barely moves past that fact, and it doesn't need to. The opening claim comes back again and again. The last line is that same claim said twice, like someone saying it over to themselves. Then comes the plain wish at the center: "I don't wanna be / Anywhere but here." Songs about wanting usually want something else. This one wants nothing more. The catch sits in its own words, though. A red hour is a sunset, and sunsets don't stay. The contentment is real, and so is the knowledge that the light is already leaving. The song lets both stand. When the voice catches on "be-be-be," it sounds less like ornament and more like trying to hold on to a moment by stretching it.

The band plays like they're in no hurry either. The drums are loose and syncopated, with a snare that sits up front. Paul Bender's walking bass line moves under clean, arpeggiated electric guitar while keyboard chords hold steady underneath. The tempo is moderate and the swing is slight, laid back without going slack. Nai Palm sings alone, breathy and smooth, with no harmonies stacked on top, which keeps the room feeling like one person's space.

That ease was earned the fun way. Bender came up with the riff on the fly during the band's Rio de Janeiro sessions for *Mood Valiant*, their first album in six years. NME reports they cut it at 2am after heaps of tea and cachaça. Drummer Perrin Moss said it came together faster than any Hiatus Kaiyote song ever had. That fits a Melbourne band known for jumping between neo-soul, jazz-funk and future soul. Here they drop the showy side and let a groove sit, the moody end of their catalog where nothing has to prove anything.

It's a song about being exactly where you want to be just as the light runs out, and it makes you want to stay in that room with it.
