A palm-muted guitar riff, distorted and fast, chugs under **High on Weed** like an engine that won't idle down. The bass runs alongside it with its own driving line, the snare and kick sit up front, and a steady hi-hat keeps everything locked. The band stays tight and keeps pushing.

That drive is the joke's delivery system. Waylon Napadogan is a musician and comedian from New Brunswick, and he markets his songs on TikTok as comedy and country. This one Apple Music files under Rock, and you can hear why: the riff owes more to a garage than to a honky-tonk. But the songwriting instinct is the same one running through his catalogue, which reads like a list of things a Maritime guy might say out loud, own, or get stuck in: "Oh Ya No For Sure Bud," "I Seen a Sasquatch," "No Wonder Sharon Took the Kids and Left My Ass Again," "Stuck in a Porta Potty," "98 Pontiac Sunfire," "2008 Ford Ranger." The titles are the punchlines and the premises at once. "High on Weed" is as blunt as any of them. It names a state of being and doesn't apologize for it.

The vocal is clear, mid-range, a little nasal, with no harmonies stacked behind it. It's conversational and rhythmic, riding the beat like someone telling a story at speed rather than performing one. The production is dry and tight, a little raw, with none of the gloss that would make it sound like it was built for anything but the song itself. Napadogan is his own label here, and the record sounds like it: direct, unfussed, made to land on a phone speaker and get shared.

There's a feeling underneath the bit worth taking seriously. Napadogan has said he wrote "Frig Off Bud," probably his oldest song, at seven, as a defence against school bullies. That's a kid learning that a funny song can be armour, that you can say the rough thing first and set the terms. He carried that instinct onto the America's Got Talent stage with an original about naming a baby Gary, and into an audience built on TikTok and Instagram. "High on Weed" comes out of the same place: the plain pleasure of saying the unpolished thing out loud and watching people laugh with you instead of at you.

It sticks because the band plays it completely straight, so you're grinning at the title and nodding along to the riff before you've decided whether it's a joke.
