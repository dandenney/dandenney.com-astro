The trick of **I Love You** is that it keeps its title honest for longer than you expect. It opens as a straight devotional, the kind of pledge that reaches past the grave ("to be here loving you when I'm in the tomb"), and only as the language turns do you realise who it's addressed to. Grian Chatten has said he wanted to take a cliché title and topic and make it his own, and that it turned out to be another song about Ireland. He also called it the first overtly political song Fontaines D.C. had written.

The love is real, and that's what makes the rest hurt. Chatten has said he left Ireland, now lives in a country responsible for much of its chaos, and feels guilty for going. You can hear that guilt in the shape of the lyric. The narrator who swears devotion is the same one who admits he clambered up, met their demands, and kept the spoils for himself. Pride and shame share a verse and never settle. Then comes the line that drops the mask: "this island's run by sharks with children's bones stuck in their jaws." It points at the 2017 discovery of the remains of 800 children at a former mother and baby home, and it lands hard. Later, the love song comes back sour and funny at once: "I loved you like a penny loves the pocket of a priest." Affection and accusation fused into one simile.

The music keeps its footing under all of it. A clean electric guitar picks arpeggios over a walking bass line and a drum kit with a firm snare, and the groove sits at a moderate, driving pace. A second, distorted guitar holds long notes behind it. The room sound is natural, and the voice is mostly single-tracked: mid-range, a little raw, half sung and half spoken, closer to someone talking at you than performing. That plainness suits the words. Nothing is dressed up, so the anger has nowhere to hide.

The song grew out of a jam. Drummer Tom Coll stepped away to collect a Deliveroo order, the three guitars kept going, and Chatten started his rant with no drums behind him. Dan Carey, who produced the band's first two records too, made Skinty Fia with them in London in 2021, at Angelic Studios. It's Irish post-punk, still angry, but turned toward grief for a home you can't stop loving and can't stand to live in.

A year after the album came out, the band was still closing every show with it, and Chatten sees it as a grounding finale of hope, which tells you how he hears the anger. It's a love song that won't let love off the hook. By the time "the bastard walks by" has come around again, you understand you can rage at a place precisely because you can't let it go.
