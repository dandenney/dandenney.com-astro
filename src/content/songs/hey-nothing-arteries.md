"I kinda hope your house explodes" gets sung twice in a row, and the repetition is both the joke and the wound. The first time it sounds like a sulk. The second time it sounds like someone checking whether they meant it. That's the trick of **Arteries**, the closing track on hey, nothing's major-label debut *Hound*: petty lines held up long enough that they start to look like devotion.

hey, nothing are Tyler Mabry and Harlow Phillips, a duo out of Athens, Georgia, best known for the viral "i haunt ur dreams." Their music sits where emo-flavored pop-punk meets indie folk, with Midwest emo in the bloodline, and "Arteries" leans on all of it. A bright acoustic guitar strums over a bass line that mostly sits on the root, and the drums push a driving mid-tempo beat with the snare landing hard on two and four. It's campfire chords bolted onto a pop-punk chassis. The lead vocal stands alone, no harmonies stacked on it, with a strained edge that keeps the earnestness from going smooth.

The song was the first thing fans heard from *Hound*. Press framed it as the record's account of the duo's growing pains, a song that asks what the friendship would have been if they had actually broken up. Read the lyrics that way and they snap into focus. The verses are small physical actions: dragging feet, an ear pressed to a door, medicine taken to go, a bike out on the road. The second pass scrambles them. Feet go against the door and the ear gets slammed into the floor, as if the same afternoon got replayed angrier. The refusal moves by one word, too. "I'll try to be the one you want," the song offers, and then the old "never" comes right back to cancel it.

The chorus is the argument itself, a long, tangled sentence about how hard it is to say the thing that hurts when you know the other person would say it right back. The answer comes in the plainest image here: "It's in my arteries." That isn't a decision. It's a fact about blood. One review heard a Better Oblivion Community Center duet feel in the song, and the comparison fits the way it treats two people's resentment as a shared language.

At over five minutes it's the longest track on *Hound*, and it takes its time. The band called the video, directed by Spencer Kelly, a picture of their "forever growing (and glowing) friendship and commitment towards one another." The song cuts off mid-phrase on that last "arteries" and fades into gentle piano to close the album, a soft landing after all that wishing for fire.

It sticks because it lets you say the meanest thing you've ever thought about someone you love and hear, underneath it, that you're never leaving.
