There are no harmonies on Willie Watson's **Dancing On My Own**. He sings it alone. What's there is one fingerpicked acoustic guitar with a clear, ringing tone and prominent bass notes doing the work a kick drum might otherwise do. Over it, Watson sings in a warm voice with a slight rasp around the edges. It's a slow, steady performance, and its plainness is the first thing that hits you.

The song belongs to Robyn. She wrote it with Patrik Berger and released it in 2010. Berger produced it, with Robyn as co-producer. A cover from an old-time specialist wasn't news. As one 2020 write-up put it, the surprise was that he went to a Swedish disco singer for material. Watson came up as a founding member of Old Crow Medicine Show, spent about 17 years recording and touring with them, then went solo in 2011. His solo work sits in the American folk and old-time line of Lead Belly, Harry Smith's anthology and string bands. His first solo album, *Folk Singer, Vol. 1*, was produced by David Rawlings and recorded at Woodland Sound Studios in Nashville.

This one he made alone in his living room. Acony Records framed the single as his answer to the collective self-isolation of spring 2020, sparked by all the live-streaming he'd been doing from home. A video of him playing it went up on April 8, about five weeks before the release. That context changes what the title means. In 2010 it was the name of a pop hit. In 2020 it described almost everyone's evening.

The recording shows where it was made. The room sounds natural and medium-sized, with no coloring added, and the guitar and voice sit apart from each other cleanly. It feels raw more than polished, like a take rather than a production. The fingerpicking keeps a gentle, flowing motion, and the low notes give it a steady forward lean without any drums. Watson doesn't sell the song. He sings it melodically and directly, with unhurried phrasing, and he lets the loneliness come through in how little is going on around him.

What comes through isn't despair. It's closer to company: one person in a room playing someone else's song, and making the quiet around it feel shared.

It sticks because a dance-floor song, sung plainly in a small room, sounds like people being alone together, and you can hear the room.
