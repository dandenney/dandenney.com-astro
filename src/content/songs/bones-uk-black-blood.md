The quotation marks are the giveaway. In **Black Blood**, Bones UK put scare quotes around "cool" and "being loved," two ideas the narrator waves off as not for them. That punctuation is armor. It's how you say a thing out loud while making sure nobody can accuse you of wanting it.

The duo of vocalist Rosie Bones and guitarist Carmen Vandenberg described the song as being about the inability to be loved by people who want to love you, especially in a normal relationship, and about destroying the chances that might let it happen. That second part is the sharper one. This isn't a song about loneliness that just happens. It's about the narrator wrecking things on purpose and calling it freedom. "I can't kiss without biting" is a confession dressed up as a boast. "I'll let you stay but I won't let you sleep" might be the cruelest and most honest line on the record: the door stays open, and the room stays uninhabitable. Pride and grief sit right next to each other. The narrator brags about being the hardest of the hard ones, then admits to turning into stone by degrees, as if the hardness is still being made and still costs something.

The music shows the hurt the words keep denying. A clean electric guitar picks out an arpeggiated figure with a slight tremolo shimmer, and a distorted guitar sustains underneath it. The groove is slow, a long way from the riff-heavy, industrial-edged attack the duo are known for, with a prominent kick and snare and a bass that keeps things simple. Rosie Bones sings with a clear, slightly raspy voice in a sustained, almost mournful way. The production stays clean and polished, with a medium-sized room's worth of reverb. The texture is full without getting crowded, so the voice never has to fight for space.

Vandenberg has called it her favourite of the songs they've written so far. Its video borrowed the stripped-back look of Nirvana's MTV Unplugged and set it in a fantasy world, with an audience of freaks and creatures. The song also turned up again, acoustic, on their 2020 Unplugged EP. Underneath the bite, it's a torch song.

Near the end, the refrain comes around again and again, telling everyone not to waste their time on the narrator, and the more often it returns the less it sounds like a warning and the more it sounds like a plea. That's why it sticks: you hear someone push love away and realize they're asking you to see through it.
