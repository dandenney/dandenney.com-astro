Three lines of the hook in **Lottery** open with the same word: *if*. "If I let you put your hands all over me," the hook begins, and the whole record hangs on that conditional. Nothing has happened yet. The man in question hasn't won anything. He's holding a ticket, and the narrator is deciding whether it pays out. That small grammatical choice turns what could be a straight come-on into a song about leverage, about who gets to say yes and what that yes is worth.

LU KALA, the Congolese-Canadian singer-songwriter who moved to Canada at three, told Entertainment Tonight she started writing that hook in March 2022. Almost a year later the song came out, and it became her first entry on the Billboard Hot 100 and the Canadian Hot 100. It's a fitting debut for her, because the hook is the song's spine. Latto comes in laughing, calling herself the biggest, and spends her verses spinning the metaphor until it nearly breaks: "Play him like a scratch off," she says, measuring a lover the way you'd scratch a lottery ticket.

The pleasure here is pride, loud and unembarrassed. But there's a real tension underneath that the song never settles. A lottery is pure luck. Yet the second verse insists on the opposite: "You gotta work for this, can't purchase this," it warns, demanding that he prove himself worthy. So is he lucky, or did he earn it? The song wants both, and that contradiction is the most human thing about it. It's the voice of someone who knows her own value and also knows how often people try to shortcut their way to it. Notice, too, what else the hook puts on the table next to the body: a look at what's on her mind, and the fantasies she might share. The real jackpot, the song hints, is being let inside her head, and that's the part held back hardest.

Uproxx called the beat groovy and disco-inspired, and Latto said the video's look followed its 70s disco flavor. Director Chandler Lass put the two singers in disco and casino sets and sent them through Las Vegas in a limo, which makes the metaphor literal. It also extends where Latto went on her 2022 album *777*, which she described as experimenting with pop, R&B and trap. Dr. Luke is among the producers, and the writing room was crowded, with nine credited writers, LU KALA among them. The pair performed it together at Billboard Women in Music in March 2023.

The song sticks because it lets you borrow a feeling most people rarely say out loud: the certainty that you are the prize, and the other person had better know it.
