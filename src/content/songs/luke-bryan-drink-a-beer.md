The person in **Drink A Beer** gets the worst phone call of their life and does the only thing that makes sense: "So I just hung up the phone." No speech, no casserole. Just a walk that ends at the water and a decision not to go home yet. That's the whole plot, and the song never asks for more.

Jim Beavers and Chris Stapleton wrote it, and the story of how is worth knowing. Beavers came into the session with the chorus already finished: sit on the pier, watch the sun go down, drink a beer. Stapleton says he pushed to make it darker, to make it about someone who had died. That instinct changes everything. The same small ritual that could have been a lazy-evening song becomes what a person does when the alternative is falling apart. The beer isn't celebration or even escape. It's something to hold while the news sinks in.

The verses are honest about how badly grief fits the comforts people offer. The narrator reaches for the standard line, that the good Lord knows the reasons, then hangs a weak "I guess" on it and admits "I can't make it all make sense." Faith and confusion sit next to each other and neither one wins. The narrator doesn't reject the greater plan, just can't feel it right now, and the song lets that stay unsettled.

The real turn comes in the last section. After the goodbye to a friend, the chorus comes back, but now it's attached to "all the times that we used to." The pier was their spot. Every chorus before that was the narrator sitting alone in a place built for two, and only at the end do you learn it. Saying goodbye and keeping the habit turn out to be the same act.

For Luke Bryan, the song hit somewhere real. He lost his brother Chris in 1997 and his sister Kelly in 2007, and he said that when the song came to him by email, it "tore me to pieces." The Beavers and Stapleton demo was just guitar and voice, with Stapleton singing it in one take. Jeff Stevens produced Bryan's version, and Stapleton sang harmony on it, so one of the writers is still present on the finished record.

It's a Nashville grief ballad from an artist known for "Country Girl (Shake It For Me)," and Bryan said it showed another depth to his artistry beyond the party guy. He called it "the coolest sad song ever." It went to No. 1 on country radio anyway, maybe because nearly everyone has a version of that pier.

The song sticks because it understands that when words fail, you go back to the place and repeat the small thing you used to share, and that counts as mourning too.
