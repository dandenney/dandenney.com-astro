The joke lands before the hurt does. "But I'm tough, so I'm just gonna cry in bed for free," Gracie Lawrence sings, and the line works both ways. It's a punchline about skipping therapy, and it's also a picture of someone alone at night, choosing the cheaper kind of falling apart. That double edge runs through all of **i'm confident that i'm insecure**. The humor isn't a way out of the feeling. It's how the feeling gets said out loud at all.

Gracie started writing it on a stressed-out night during a low stretch of the pandemic. She played it for her brother Clyde, then for bandmates Jonny Koh and Jordan Cohen and for Jon Bellion, who helped shape it. You can hear that origin in the lyric's shape. The verses spill like a late-night text to yourself, half self-diagnosis and half self-roast. Then, tucked into the second verse, comes the plainest sentence in the song: "But I need to know that if I die that you'll be proud of me." It arrives right after she claims she's not deep and doesn't want to know what death is about. The denial and the need sit next to each other, and the song never pretends one cancels the other.

The music holds all of this gently. Piano chords and a walking bass line set a medium, slightly swung groove, the drums are brushed, and strings come in with sustained chords that warm the room without crowding it. Gracie's voice is clear with a little breath around the edges, so the confessions come across as conversation, not performance. It's clean, polished pop-soul. Clyde and Gracie grew up on Stevie Wonder, Randy Newman and Aretha Franklin records, and the Randy Newman part shows most here: the wry, specific line that turns out to hide real pain.

Later the lens widens. Pisces jokes give way to a list of famous things doubting themselves, a Jersey Shore pun among them, and the best of the list is "And I know that Ringo sometimes wishes he was Paul." It's silly, and it's also generous. Clyde has said the song rests on the idea that everyone carries some mix of confidence and insecurity, and that list is where the private confession becomes company. A parenthetical line spells it out: everybody gets insecure sometimes.

Lawrence put this out the day before opening a stadium tour with the Jonas Brothers at Yankee Stadium. Releasing a song about being knocked flat by criticism right before walking onto stadium stages is its own small act of the song's title.

The song gets you to laugh at your own worst nighttime thoughts, then quietly admits they're real, so you feel less alone with yours.
