**LosT** opens on a scene that would be funny if it weren't so bleak: someone in front of Evangelion, high on ketamine, admitting they lost the plot a while ago. Then comes the inventory. The dog died. The friends hate them. There's a glimpse of themselves on MTV. And a line that rhymes like a joke and lands like a confession: "my ego is not my amigo." Bring Me The Horizon's Oli Sykes has called the song "more honest" and likened it to "a therapy session with myself," and that's the right frame. It's a list of grievances that slowly turns into a diagnosis.

What makes it hit is how much the song refuses to choose between laughing and drowning. The verses joke about serotonin and imaginary enemies, but the humor is self-defense, and it cracks. "The next time that I open up to someone will be my autopsy" is a punchline about a doctor who tried to section the narrator, and it's also a promise never to trust anyone again. Bitterness and fear sit together, unresolved. The chorus drops the wit entirely. The medicine isn't working, there's no remedy, and the only question left is why. The dead dog has been read as Sykes' Oskar, who died in 2021, and the rest as disillusionment with therapy and medication. The most physical image is the smallest: "Red crescent moons all over my hands," nail marks from clenched fists, pain turned inward where nobody sees it.

The sound matches that pressure. A melodic, slightly distorted vocal floats over a synth pad, then a distorted guitar riff and punchy drums crash in at a fast clip. The kick and snare sit right up front, and cymbals splash around them. The voice goes raw and strained, stacked with harmonies, while a synth melody surfaces above the guitars. Zakk Cervini and Evil Twin produced it polished and wide, but the scream still sounds like it's coming apart at the seams. This is the third single from *Post Human: Nex Gen*, where the band adds hyper-pop electronics to mainstream rock, and Sykes has said he wants them "unhinged" again, closer to the band before they got big. You can hear that here: glossy production around a person losing it.

The lyrics also shrink to a single warning, "If I keep this up / I think I'm gonna break down," repeated until it sounds less like a threat than a forecast.

It sticks because it gives you a chorus you can shout along with about the exact thing you can't say out loud to anyone: that you've tried the fixes, and you still don't know what's wrong with you.
