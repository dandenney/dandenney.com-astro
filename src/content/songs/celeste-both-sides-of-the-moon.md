The detail that cracks **Both Sides Of The Moon** open is a smell. "I smelled the cinnamon, how could I depend?" Celeste sings, and the whole song turns on that one sensory clue: a scent that isn't hers, carried on someone she loves. It's an oddly domestic way to discover betrayal, and that's why it lands. Nobody in this song finds a letter or catches a lie outright. The narrator just knows, the way you know, and then has to live inside the knowing.

What makes the lyric hard to shake is how many feelings it refuses to sort out. Suspicion has already "proved me right," yet the next breath is "Did I deserve it?", self-blame arriving right on the heels of being wronged. Later she's "pacing back with him," pain in the chest, still telling herself "I'm hoping they're just friends." That line is the saddest one in the song because the narrator doesn't believe it and says it anyway. The pronouns slip, too: "you" in one line, "his" in the next, as if she can't decide whether to talk about this person or to them. The chorus explains why she can't leave. "And the moon is a slave to the tides" flips the usual physics, making the moon the thing being pulled, and that's exactly how this love feels from the inside: something that should be in charge, dragged around by forces it can't refuse. "Both sides" works twice over, seeing the bright face and the dark one, and being stuck arguing both cases in your own head.

The music doesn't dramatize any of this, which is the point. A slow, laid-back groove sits under a tight, dry snare. The bass walks simply, sustained keyboard chords hang in the air, and a clean electric guitar shimmers in arpeggios. Celeste's voice is breathy with a little rasp, delivered smooth and sustained, so the hurt comes out composed. There's a lot of room around every instrument.

The people in that room matter. Gotts Street Park, a Leeds band, two of whose members met on a jazz course at Leeds College of Music, say they wrote this and "Lately" with Celeste in two days in Josh Crocker's basement setup in Leeds, after she'd asked to jam with a live band while still almost unknown. Crocker produced and played bass, organ and piano; Gordon Nicholl drums, Joe Harris guitar, Tom Henry keys, with horns from Todd Simon and Juliane Gralle. It's British jazz-soul made by players, released first as a live single in 2018, then folded into the deluxe edition of *Not Your Muse*, the album that took Celeste to number one.

The song lets you sit inside the moment you already know the truth and still can't make yourself stop hoping.
