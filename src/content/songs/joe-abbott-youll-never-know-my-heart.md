A song called **You'll Never Know My Heart** ought to sound guarded, and this one doesn't. Two voices sing it, sometimes in close harmony and sometimes in unison, over a brushed shuffle that never hurries. The title is a closed door. The sound is two people standing in the same doorway, breathing on the same beat. That gap between the words on the label and the warmth in the room is where the song lives, and it's worth sitting in.

Joe Abbott wrote it and sings lead, with a clear, resonant voice that doesn't push. Cat Clyde comes in on harmony and blends so well that the seams almost disappear. She doesn't decorate his line so much as lean into it. Underneath, a fingerpicked guitar carries the melody, and Isaac Story's upright bass walks steadily from chord to chord. Paul Townsend keeps the shuffle going on brushes, relaxed and unhurried. The room feels medium-sized and warm, polished but still natural.

The lineage makes sense once you see who's involved. Abbott is a Vancouver songwriter and multi-instrumentalist who has worked as a sideman with Petunia and the Vipers and with Harpdog Brown, and he has lived everywhere from New Orleans to Montreal. His music sits in the folk and trad-country tradition that now often gets called Americana. Clyde's own records draw on blues, soul, jump, jazz and swing. Put them together and you get country-folk with a swing pulse in its legs, a duet where trading verses matters less than the sound of two voices finding the same pitch. The session was cut at Flash Recording in Vancouver with Abbott's band, The Flying J's, and William Poulin producing. Abbott also plays electric guitar and clarinet on the track, and Liam McIvor Tyner plays steel guitar.

It was the last of three singles ahead of Abbott's debut LP, *Duster*, where it sits third. The pair also toured together, and the ease shows. Nobody is trying to win the song.

What stays with you is the contradiction. A title that insists on distance gets sung as a duet, so the feeling comes across less as a wall than as a confession told to someone already close enough to hear it. Whether that heart is being hidden or offered, the two voices don't force an answer.

The song makes "you'll never know" sound like something said warmly to the one person who already does, and that tenderness is the hook you keep coming back for.
