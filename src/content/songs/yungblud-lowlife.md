The chorus is a refusal dressed as a party: **Lowlife** wants to "stay right here in my underwear," and it hollers the plan over a driving, distorted guitar riff and a fast live beat with a hard snare. It sounds like a gang shout. It's really one person who can't face the door.

YUNGBLUD has said he wrote the song because he didn't want to leave his house. He wanted to be nothing, he said, and craved a specific kind of boredom: the same sheets, mind-numbing TV. That's a strange thing to build a sing-along on, and it's why the song works. The verses come at you fast, almost spoken, in a raw, slightly strained voice that sounds dry and close. Then the delivery swings into something melodic and anthemic, with a second voice harmonizing. The production stays unpolished, which suits a song about not bothering to clean up.

Listen past the swagger and the verses keep confessing. He admits he's embarrassed most days, walking around Camden with a smirk on his face. The next move is "Buy a bottle of gin to get the memories erased." The smirk and the gin are the same gesture. Then comes the oddest, most disarming turn. He borrows a friend's line, "Young, dumb, living off mum," and stops to own up that not all the words are his and to ask not to be judged. A song this loud about not caring keeps worrying what you'll think. Later he calls himself hypocritical and a petulant baby before telling someone to shut up. He seems to know exactly how the act looks, and he won't drop it.

He co-wrote and co-produced the track with Jake Torrey and Paul Meany. YUNGBLUD said he went into the studio with Meany with no references and started each session from a blank canvas and a beat. He wasn't chasing "Close To Me" or "Life On Mars." The label framed it as a return to his alternative roots, and the pop-punk bones show: the riff, the snare, the "la-la-la" chants built for a crowd to fill in. The emo streak is in the self-diagnosis. It also shows in the video, which he pitched as "emo Shameless" and shot in Camden, walking beside his younger self.

The lyrics end on a message to mum. He's skipping Sunday dinner because he thinks he's had too much to drink. He signs off "I love ya." After all the bravado about not needing saving, the last thing he does is reach out to someone at home.

The hook lets you shout your worst week as a victory chant, and then that last message quietly shows you the person who needed the chant.
