Waking up is the first crisis here. The song opens on someone surfacing from REM, headless and half-sure they've died, then hauling a body out of bed. The next move tells you everything: "Tell myself I'm okay." It isn't a statement. It's a chore, done in the same flat breath as getting on with the day, and then two words land on their own: mostly lonely.

**Slowly** is a Cleveland singer-songwriter's song about the gap between functioning and being alive, and Skuff Micksun keeps that gap open. The verses slide between "I" and "we" without warning. First the wish is that we don't want to be dark no more, then it's that I don't. One foot in the grave, no sleep in days, trouble every day. Sometimes the darkness is shared. Sometimes it's private property. The song never settles which. That restlessness is the feeling: you can't tell whether you're one person in trouble or part of a crowd in trouble, and neither answer helps much.

The long middle stretch is where the writing gets interesting. It runs on a chain of rhymes and echoes (senses, senseless, makes sense, headless, relentless, endless, "wreck-less") that sounds like a mind spinning a word until it stops meaning anything. Then it turns on itself: "Am I doing it again." That line is the confession. The narrator catches the spiral mid-spiral and names the urge to crawl out of their own skin. In the same passage they say it's all their fault, and also that it's not all bad. Blame and comfort sit side by side, and the song lets both stand.

The chorus asks for very little: a light, a spark, a smile from someone. The detail that stings is where that smile has to come from, a part of the other person that hasn't died. It's a plea for company from someone who's also barely holding on. And then the song undercuts its own modest hope: "But I'm barely alive."

Skuff Micksun tagged the acoustic version #midwestemo when he put it out, and the confession fits the label, a narrator who talks through their own bad thinking out loud. The original song's credits list Casey W. Skufca as composer and lyricist, with Tyler Cratcha also credited as composer. He promoted this version as "out everywhere to keep you warm," which is a generous way to hand someone a song this cold. That's the trick, though. Warmth here means recognition, not rescue.

What sticks is how small the ask is, and how far away it still feels: you finish the song wanting to be that one smile for somebody.
