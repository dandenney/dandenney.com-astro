The trouble starts with a math problem: "the more I see / Of life on earth, the more I wanna leave." Mikey Mike sets it up as a simple ratio, the kind of thing you'd say half-joking, and then spends the rest of **Life on Earth** proving he means it. This isn't a breakup song or a bad-day song. It's about a deeper tiredness, a sense that the place itself is wrong, and a stubborn faith that somewhere else must be right.

The chorus carries that faith. The narrator keeps asking to be taken home, which sounds like homesickness, but the home is a place he has never been, just a better one somewhere out there. That's what gives the song its odd tenderness. Exhaustion and hope sit in the same breath, and neither wins. The second verse gets angrier. He's low, watching psychopaths run the show and get rewarded for it, and the line about how far you'll go curdles into a sneer. Then the song drops the anger for something more fragile, a plea to be woken from a dream because he feels stuck. The next confession is the one that lands hardest. "I been trying, trying to sing" his way out, and the song you're hearing becomes the attempt itself. After that the chorus comes back twice, no answer found, just the plea again.

The band doesn't mope along with him. A drum kit keeps a steady, driving beat with a prominent snare. The bass holds a plain, repeating line, and a clean guitar figure picks out arpeggios around the melody. The tempo is moderate and the groove keeps pressing forward, so the music keeps moving even as the words keep asking to get off. Mikey Mike's voice is clear with a little rasp at the edges, and he sings it straight, which makes the despair sound lived-in rather than performed.

Mikey Mike is the stage name of Mike Williams, a Los Angeles singer, songwriter and producer who produced "Jump" on Rihanna's *Unapologetic* and whose debut single, "Doin' Me," was produced by Rick Rubin. He's credited as writer and producer here, with Mark Nolan as an additional producer. It was the first song shared from the album *Mikey Mike's Life on Earth: Vol. 1*, a record he began about five years before its release, a stretch in which he was signed and then dropped by a label. He describes the project as a trip through life, from spiritual places to more physical or nasty ones, and expects to keep adding volumes over his lifetime. This song is the title-card thesis for all of it.

It sticks because it lets you admit you're worn out on the world without giving up on the idea that a better one is waiting somewhere.
