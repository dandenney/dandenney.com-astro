One phone call, and the narrator of **Dust in a Baggie** spends it on his father. Not a lawyer, not a friend from the crowd that got him here. "I used my only phone call to contact my daddy," Billy Strings sings, and the next line lands like a gavel: "I got 20 long years for some dust in a baggie." The phrasing does a lot of work. "Daddy" is a kid's word, reached for by a grown man who has nobody else. "Dust" makes the drug sound like almost nothing, which is the point. A lifetime traded for a speck in a plastic bag.

Strings has said he wrote it in about 30 minutes at work, with no guitar, just a melody in his head and a little notepad. He told Theo Von it's about Brad Lasco, the man the *Me/And/Dad* record is dedicated to, who had died a couple of years earlier. Strings has also described the song as being about a man who ruined his life and sits in prison wondering where he went wrong. Some traditional bluegrass fans dismissed it as a song about meth. They missed the shame. The verses move from the binge (no sleep in seven days, the lighter under the bulb) to the regret of not listening to "mom and papa," and then to the line that strips the man down: "In here where they call me by a number, not a name." He isn't defiant and he isn't asking for pity. He's embarrassed, and he knows he's back "once again," so the regret comes with a suspicion it won't stick.

The band plays it fast and bright. On this 2016 re-recording, the closing track of his self-titled EP, cut in Nashville at Minutia Studios, the banjo snaps out quick, percussive runs, and Casey Campbell's mandolin weaves fills around the vocal. Acoustic guitar keeps a steady rhythm and Erik Alvar's upright bass walks underneath. The room sounds clean and open. Strings sings in a clear, slightly raspy voice, conversational, more like someone telling you what happened than performing a lament. That plainness keeps the despair from going syrupy.

It's a player raised on Doc Watson, Bill Monroe and Larry Sparks writing a song that could pass for an old one, with meth at the center. The song first appeared on *Rock of Ages*, his 2013 record with Don Julin. Later it became a live staple. A basement video, shot while Strings and friends were on mushrooms, had passed 29 million views by early 2024, and a 2023 Mohegan Sun performance opens *Live Vol. 1*.

The picking races ahead like nothing's wrong, and that's the trap: you're tapping your foot right up until you realize you're keeping time with a man counting out twenty years.
