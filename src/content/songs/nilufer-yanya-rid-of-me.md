The scariest line in **Rid of Me** is also the most tender one. "I beg you, my darling," the narrator sings, and a few breaths later she promises, "I'm gonna twist your head off, see." The song doesn't pick between those two people. It's a plea and a threat from the same mouth, and the pull of it comes from how little distance there is between them.

PJ Harvey wrote it, and her 1993 alt-rock original is the template. Nilüfer Yanya's version keeps that bones-out obsession but changes the temperature. A clean electric guitar picks out an arpeggiated figure with a little reverb on it, a second guitar joins, and the tempo sits at a medium, driving pace that keeps pressing forward. Yanya sings clear and slightly breathy, close to the ear, with no harmonies to soften it. The room sounds natural and a bit raw. The menace doesn't come from volume. It comes from how composed everything sounds while the words get stranger. Critics have called the version largely faithful to Harvey's, and it was produced by Jazzi Bobbi, Yanya's longtime bandmate.

Underneath the bodily images and the threats sits a plain confession: "I've been lonely above everything." That line explains the rest. This is someone so afraid of being left that love turns into holding a person in place. The refrain, repeated until it feels like a loop the narrator can't escape, asks whether the lover wishes they'd never met her. It's cruel and wounded at once. She's taunting them, and she's also saying out loud what she fears they already think.

Yanya has called the song defiant, alien and twisted, and also very romantic, despite what some of the lyrics get at. That's the right way to hear it. A teacher showed her Harvey's track when she was 13 or 14, and she said it took her years to really understand it. She played it on tour behind *Painless* before this recording came out, and she has said she sometimes feels more comfortable playing it than her own songs. You can hear that ease in her direct delivery. She isn't acting out the obsession. She's describing it from the inside, like someone who knows the feeling and isn't ashamed of it.

The song makes you feel how close desperate love sits to danger, and it makes you a little sorry for the person holding on too tight.
