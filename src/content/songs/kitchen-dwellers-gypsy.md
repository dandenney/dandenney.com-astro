No harmonies stack up behind the voice on **Gypsy**. Kitchen Dwellers hand the song to a single clear, mid-range voice and let it talk. The delivery is conversational, close to speech, with a slight melancholy running under it. It sounds less like a performance than like one person telling you something after everyone else has gone quiet.

The band around that voice keeps moving. Acoustic guitar strums a steady pattern, the banjo runs a quick, plucked melodic line, and the mandolin answers it in counterpoint. Underneath, the bass walks. The tempo is moderate and the groove is relaxed but driving, so the song rolls forward even as the voice holds something back.

That's where the feeling lives, in the gap between a band that's going somewhere and a voice that sounds a little low. The two don't reconcile. The pickers stay bright and busy, the singing stays plain and a bit heavy, and the song lets both be true at once. Motion and sadness share the same frame, and neither one wins.

Torrin Daniels, the band's lead singer and banjo player, is credited as the songwriter. Kitchen Dwellers came together at Montana State, sit in the jamgrass scene, and fans have taken to calling what they do "Galaxy Grass." The song comes from *Ghost in the Bottle*, their second studio album and their first release since a 2013 debut. Daniels said the band had changed a lot since it last recorded and wanted the record to sound like who they were now. They cut it in two Colorado studios, Mountain Star in Nederland and Desert Rose in Durango, with Grammy-winning engineer A.G. Lunsford mixing and mastering.

The recording is dry and intimate, like a small room with the doors shut. It's clean and unadorned. A dry sound leaves a voice nowhere to hide, which suits a song that rests so much on one singer. At close to six minutes, it takes its time, which fits a band from the jamgrass world.

What makes **Gypsy** stick is that the band keeps carrying you down the road while the lone voice sounds like it's carrying the weight for everyone, so you feel how a person can keep moving and still be heavy-hearted.
