The chorus line on **It Won't Last** is a brag that doesn't hold up: "The best thing about this feeling is it won't last." Matt James sings it like a man who has already made peace with the morning, but everything around that line argues the other way. The same chorus opens with "Why can't it just stay the same?" The narrator is calling the ending a mercy while begging for one more hour, and the song never settles which one he means.

James has said the song is about a hookup, an intense connection that turns out by daylight to have been an intoxicating moment. The lyric fills that out with small, tired details. There's whiskey left in the bottle. Two people stare at each other in silence. Both have had too much, and he could do with another. The pleading shows up where he tries to sound noble. "What Kind of man would I be if I didn't hold you?" frames tenderness as an obligation. Right after, he reminds her he warned her he'd leave, so the leaving is already her fault. By the second verse he offers to stay as long as she'll let him, then undercuts the offer: "So just try to forget me." That's a man asking to be let off the hook and hoping not to be. The song lands its last word on the title phrase, repeated alone, as if he's still talking himself into it.

The music holds that tension without spelling it out. A clean electric guitar rolls through arpeggiated chords, open and patient, over a bass that sits on the root and a steady beat with a prominent snare. The tempo is moderate and the groove drives, so the song keeps moving even while the words want time to stop. James has a raw, bluesy edge in the mid-range, and second-voice harmonies slip in on some phrases. The room sounds natural and the production is polished. Philip Mosley produced and engineered *Under the Sun*, the Palestine, Texas band's fan-funded third album.

James called the song very guitar driven, and the band wanted its video, shot at the Bodega bar in Fort Worth, to showcase guitarists Ryan Kiefer and Chuck Wepfer. That focus fits. *Under the Sun* was Wepfer's first record with the band. The track sits in Texas hard rock and post-grunge, with bluesy touches. The band's earlier *Burn the Ships* was produced by Muscle Shoals producer Jimmy Johnson. It sounds built for a bar like the one in the video, a place to drown out second thoughts.

The song works because you can hear the narrator insisting the ending is the good part, and you don't believe him for a second.
