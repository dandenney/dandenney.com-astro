**Baby** opens with an ultimatum: "You're killing me so finish me off or spare me." Then Born Without Bones swerves into something smaller and harder to bear. Scott Ayotte starts taking attendance on a life he's watching from far away. Where's Nicole? Is Priscilla ordering Chinese? Is Wayne working through the wine bottles everyone brought? It's the jealousy of someone picturing a party that goes on fine without him. The details are so ordinary they sting, because ordinary is exactly what he's lost.

The memories come in the same plain register: sex on the living room floor every Sunday, the smell of a log cabin, small hands reaching for his. Then the line where it curdles: "I wonder what they're clasped around now." It reads as tenderness and works as suspicion. He can't think about her hands without imagining someone else holding them. Later, a bed that sinks in the middle, a mismatch of bodies, waking up covered in lint. Ayotte keeps reaching for physical evidence, as if proving the closeness was real might bring it back.

What keeps the song honest is that he doesn't only grieve. "But I don't miss not hearing my name" admits the relationship was starving him too, that he wanted more and kept getting the same. Then he folds anyway and tells her she's got him now. Clear-eyed and hopelessly hooked sit in the same breath, and neither wins. The last stretch of lyrics goes big, living and dying, a life he calls meaningless without her light. That's the way heartbreak does at 21, which is how old Ayotte says he was when the band cut the record.

The arrangement is built from plain parts. A fingerpicked acoustic pattern circles under a clean, sustained electric chord and a bass that sticks to root notes. The tempo is moderate and the groove is relaxed. Ayotte sings clear and mid-range, alone, with no harmonies to soften it. Then the drums come in with a driving beat, and his delivery moves from melodic to belted. Born Without Bones come out of Massachusetts DIY punk and get tagged midwest emo, and the song fits that shape: a quiet confession that can't stay quiet, with heartland storytelling in the specifics.

Ayotte says he wrote "Baby" before anything else on the album, and the band had never played it live when they recorded it in 2012 at Magpie Cage in Baltimore, with J. Robbins engineering. He's called it almost a meme song now. They re-recorded it in 2021 for the Pictures of the Sun EP, and toured the album's tenth anniversary in 2023.

It sticks because it asks the question you're ashamed to ask after a breakup, what their hands are holding now, and then lets you shout it.
